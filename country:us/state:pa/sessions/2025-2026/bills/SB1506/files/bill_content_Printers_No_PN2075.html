AN ACT
Providing for consumer rights in digital games; imposing a
penalty; and providing for severability.
The General Assembly finds and declares that:
(1) Consumers increasingly purchase digital games rather
than physical copies and often pay full retail prices
comparable to or exceeding those of physical media.
(2) Many digital games depend on services controlled by
a publisher or developer, including authentication systems,
server access, digital rights management and required
software updates, without which the game cannot be used.
(3) When an operator ceases to provide those services,
consumers who paid to acquire a game may lose all ability to
use it, notwithstanding a reasonable expectation of continued
access established at the time of purchase.
(4) Consumers are frequently given little or no advance
notice before losing access and are left without a functional
product, a means of continued use or a refund.
(5) It is the intent of the General Assembly to protect

Section 1. Short title.
This act shall be known and may be cited as the Protect Our
Games Act.
Section 2. Definitions.
The following words and phrases when used in this act shall
have the meanings given to them in this section unless the
context clearly indicates otherwise:
"Digital game." A game that a person accesses and
manipulates using a specialized electronic gaming device,
computer, mobile device, tablet or other device with a display
screen, including any add-on or additional content for the game.
"Digital game operator." A publisher, developer or other
person that controls whether a purchaser of a digital game can
make ordinary use of the digital game, including, but not
limited to, controlling authentication systems, server access,
digital rights management or required software updates. The term
does not include a person that provides general-purpose hosting,
cloud computing, storage, network services or an online platform
or marketplace for the distribution of a digital game but does
not control whether a purchaser may make ordinary use of the
digital game.
"Ordinary use." A purchaser's ability to use the core
features of a digital game consistent with the reasonable
expectations of a purchaser based on how the digital game was

Section 3. Notice, continued use and refund requirements.
(a) Notice.--The following shall apply to a digital game
first available for purchase or rereleased for purchase on or
after January 1, 2028:
(1) Sixty days before a digital game operator ceases to
provide services necessary for the ordinary use of the
digital game, the operator shall communicate the following to
each purchaser or prospective purchaser of the digital game:
(i) The date on which services necessary for the
ordinary use of the digital game will cease.
(ii) Any services that will no longer be provided by
the operator.
(iii) Any game features that will no longer be
available to the purchaser.
(iv) Any known security risks that may result from
the cessation of services.
(v) How the purchaser can continue to use the
digital game or obtain a refund in accordance with
subsection (b)(3).
(2) A digital game operator shall communicate the
information required under paragraph (1) by:
(i) notifying purchasers directly through the
operator's digital game; or
(ii) posting the information on the operator's
publicly accessible Internet website.
(b) Requirement for continued use.--Beginning on the date
that a digital game operator ceases to provide services
necessary for the ordinary use of a digital game, the operator

(d) Nonapplicability.--This section does not apply to the
following:
(1) Any subscription-based service that advertises or
offers for sale access to any digital game solely for the
duration of the subscription.
(2) Any digital game that is advertised or offered to a
person for no monetary consideration, unless the person has
made purchases or invested money to upgrade characters or
unlock features within the game. If a consumer has spent $50
or more on upgrades or features for a game offered for no
monetary consideration, the gaming operator shall follow the
conditions of subsection (b).
(3) Any digital game that is advertised or offered to a
person to whom the seller cannot revoke access after the
transaction, including a digital game that has been made
available at the time of purchase for permanent offline
download to an external storage source to be used without a
connection to the Internet.
Section 4. Penalty.
A violation of this act shall be subject to a civil penalty
of $1,000 for a first violation, $5,000 for a second violation
and $10,000 for a third or subsequent violation. The violator
shall pay attorney fees and investigative costs of the Attorney
General or district attorney that brings an action under this
act.

Actions for relief under this act may be prosecuted

Section 6. Severability.
If any provision of this act, including the application of
the act to any person or circumstance, is held invalid, the
invalidity shall not affect other provisions or applications of
this act that can be given effect without the invalid provision
or application, and, to this end, the provisions of this act are
severable.
Section 7. Effective date.
This act shall take effect in 60 days.
