§ 3501. Scope of chapter.
This chapter relates to:
(1) An informal dispute resolution process for long-term
care nursing facilities.
(2) An informal dispute resolution process for
hospitals.
(3) A formal dispute resolution process for hospitals.

§ 3503. Notice of how to dispute deficiencies.
(a) Requirement.--Upon issuance of a finding of deficiency
under this chapter, the department shall provide written notice
of all available rights to dispute deficiencies, including the
rights under this chapter, to the long-term care nursing
facility under Subchapter B (relating to informal dispute
resolution for long-term care nursing facilities) or the
hospital under Subchapter C (relating to informal dispute
resolution for hospitals) or D (relating to formal dispute
resolution for hospitals) that is the subject of the deficiency.
(b) Contents.--The written notice under subsection (a) must
include a clear explanation of the procedures and the applicable
timelines to dispute deficiencies.
(c) Duty of department.--The department shall maintain a
standardized form for the notice described under this section.

§ 3513. Data collection.
T he department shall collect and maintain the following data:
(1) The total number of review requests received on an
annual basis, including the number of independent IDR
requests and the number of State informal dispute resolution
review requests.
(2) The total number of independent IDRs completed,
including the number of reviews that resulted in the removal
of a deficiency, the number of reviews that resulted in the
downgrade of a deficiency and the number of reviews that
upheld a deficiency.
(3) The total number of State informal dispute
resolution reviews completed, including the number of reviews
that resulted in the removal of a deficiency, the number of
reviews that resulted in the downgrade of a deficiency and
the number of reviews that upheld a deficiency.

§ 3531. Right to pursue.
A hospital may pursue formal dispute resolution regarding
substantial and bona fide disputes of deficiencies that are
cited during a survey and that:
(1) involve immediate jeopardy findings for open
hospitals;
(2) prevent a closed hospital from reopening;
(3) prevent a hospital that has not yet opened from
beginning operations; or
(4) involve State-assessed penalties amounting to
$10,000 or more in the aggregate.
§ 3532. Notice of intent.
A hospital that wishes to pursue formal dispute resolution
under this subchapter shall, not later than 10 days after
receiving formal communication of deficiencies, provide to the
department written notice of intent to pursue formal dispute
resolution.
§ 3533. Consolidation.
All disputes of deficiencies contested under this subchapter
by a hospital must be consolidated into a single dispute
resolution proceeding such that the hospital may not appeal each
finding as to deficiencies separately but must address all
contested matters in a single dispute resolution process.

§ 3535. Hearing.
(a) Scheduling and notice.--As soon as practicable, but not
later than 10 days after receiving a request for a hearing, the
arbitrators shall:
(1) Fix a date for the hearing.
(2) Notify both parties of the time and place of the
hearing.
(b) Purpose.--The purpose of the hearing is to:
(1) facilitate a negotiated resolution or compromise; or
(2) provide final resolution through the issuance of a
binding ruling.
(c) Conduct.--
(1) The hearing shall be conducted by all the
arbitrators but a majority may determine any issue and render
a final decision.
(2) If, during the course of the hearing, an arbitrator
for any reason ceases to act, the remaining arbitrator or
arbitrators appointed to act as neutrals may continue with
the hearing and determine the controversy.

§ 3536. Final decision.
(a) Authorization.--If a compromise cannot be reached, the
arbitrators shall be empowered to issue a decision that shall be
binding on both parties.
(b) Written and signed.--The decision of the arbitrators
shall be in writing and shall be signed by the arbitrators.
(c) Delivery.--The arbitrators shall deliver a copy of the
decision to each party personally or by registered or certified
mail, or as otherwise agreed to by the parties.
(d) Deficiencies.-- If a hospital is successful during the
formal dispute resolution process at demonstrating that
deficiencies should not have been cited, the deficiencies shall
be removed from the statement of deficiencies and any
enforcement actions imposed solely as a result of those cited
deficiencies shall be rescinded.
§ 3537. Costs.
(a) General rule.-- Except as provided in subsection (b), the
hospital involved in the formal dispute resolution process shall
pay the costs incurred in the conduct of the formal dispute
resolution process. Costs shall include the expenses and fees of
the arbitrators and other administrative expenses, except
counsel fees.
(b) Exception.--Notwithstanding subsection (a), the
arbitrators may award the costs under subsection (a) to the
hospital as the prevailing party or may allocate the costs if
the department has engaged in willful or malicious misconduct
against the hospital.
