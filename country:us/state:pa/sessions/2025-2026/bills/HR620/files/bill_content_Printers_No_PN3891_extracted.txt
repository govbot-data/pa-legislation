2025-2026 Regular Session HR 620 PN 3891 Bill Text (HTM) PRINTER'S NO. 3891 THE GENERAL ASSEMBLY OF PENNSYLVANIAHOUSE RESOLUTION No.620 Session of 2026 INTRODUCED BY PUGH, KUZMA, COOPER, GALLAGHER AND CIRESI, SEPTEMBER 18, 2026 REFERRED TO COMMITTEE ON INTERGOVERNMENTAL AFFAIRS AND OPERATIONS, SEPTEMBER 21, 2026 A RESOLUTIONUrging the Congress of the United States to adopt legislation providing screening to firefighters for sarcoidosis and other occupational diseases linked to chronic exposure to smoke, soot, PFAS, combustion byproducts, particulate matter and other fireground toxins and ensuring that our nation's firefighters receive comprehensive early detection for conditions that carry significant risks of long-term lung damage and other ailments, which require timely diagnoses for effective intervention.WHEREAS, Thousands of firefighters across the United States, including many across Pennsylvania, are routinely exposed to smoke, soot, PFAS, combustion byproducts, particulates and other hazardous fireground toxins that are strongly linked to sarcoidosis and other related inflammatory conditions; andWHEREAS, Firefighters who develop sarcoidosis and other related occupational diseases face significantly elevated risks of chronic lung inflammation, permanent organ damage, misdiagnosis due to symptom overlap with cancer and delayed treatment, demonstrating a critical gap in early detection and comprehensive health screening; andWHEREAS, Sarcoidosis is an inflammatory disease in which 123456789101112131415161718192021 small clusters of granulomas form in organs such as the lungs, lymph nodes, skin, eyes or liver, and may include fatigue, cough, shortness of breath, chest pain, swollen lymph nodes, skin lesions or rashes, eye inflammation, fever, night sweats, joint pain, lung scarring or impaired breathing; andWHEREAS, Sarcoidosis and related inflammatory conditions should be included in firefighter screening because it commonly develops from exposure to smoke, soot, PFAS, combustion byproducts and toxic particulates, which often presents with symptoms that mimic cancer, and is frequently detected incidentally on the same chest X-rays and CT scans already used in firefighter cancer screening programs; andWHEREAS, Multiple stakeholders have emphasized the urgent need for legislation, highlighting the growing prevalence of sarcoidosis and other related occupational diseases among firefighters; andWHEREAS, Pennsylvania is home to a large community of career and volunteer firefighters who respond to thousands of emergencies each year across Pennsylvania, exemplifying the longstanding history of public service in Pennsylvania and underscoring the Statewide need for comprehensive health protections, including enhanced screening for sarcoidosis and other related inflammatory conditions; andWHEREAS, Screening for sarcoidosis and other related occupational diseases helps firefighters by enabling earlier detection of conditions that often go unnoticed, reducing long-term health complications and ensuring timely access to appropriate medical evaluation and treatment;
