AN ACT
Providing for 340B drug reimbursement, for reimbursement claims
methodology, for safety net reimbursement protection, for
safety net prescription drug distribution and access
protections and for enforcement; and imposing penalties.
The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania
hereby enacts as follows:
Section 1. Short title.
This act shall be known and may be cited as the Safety Net
Nondiscrimination Act.
Section 2. Definitions.
The following words and phrases when used in this act shall
have the meanings given to them in this section unless the
context clearly indicates otherwise:
"340B drug." A drug that is a covered outpatient drug within
the meaning of 42 U.S.C. § 256b (relating to limitation on
prices of drugs purchased by covered entities), that a
manufacturer is required to offer to a covered entity at a
reduced price under 42 U.S.C. § 256b and that is purchased by a
covered entity. For the purposes of this definition, a drug

Section 4. Reimbursement claims methodology.
The department, a covered insurer, a pharmacy benefits
manager or another entity that administers or manages
prescription drug benefits on behalf of the department or a
Medicaid managed care organization shall, with respect to
payment for drugs under Medicaid, rely exclusively on a claims-
level methodology that uses a nationally recognized billing
modifier to identify drugs purchased under 42 U.S.C. § 256b
(relating to limitation on prices of drugs purchased by covered
entities) to ensure that utilization data for the drugs are
excluded from the information reported under 42 U.S.C. §
1396b(m)(2)(A)(xiii) (relating to payment to States) and from
Medicaid drug rebate requests.

(a) Prohibited conduct.--A Medicaid managed care
organization may not:
(1) Take any of the following actions with respect to a
340B drug:
(i) Prevent a covered entity or contract pharmacy
from administering or dispensing a 340B drug by any
lawful means, including mail delivery.
(ii) Prevent a covered entity or contract pharmacy
from submitting a claim for reimbursement for a 340B
drug.
(iii) Decline to cover a claim for reimbursement for
a 340B drug that was administered or dispensed.
(2) Impose a limitation on the distance between a
covered entity and a contract pharmacy or between a covered
entity or contract pharmacy and a patient.
(3) Except as necessary to comply with Federal law,
request, require or compel the transmission of ingredient
cost, pricing data or other data pertaining to the program
under 42 U.S.C. § 256b (relating to limitation on prices of
drugs purchased by covered entities).
(b) Abrogation.--A rule, regulation or statement of policy
that is inconsistent with subsection (a) is abrogated to the
extent of the inconsistency.
(c) Outpatient prescription drug coverage.--The department
shall maintain coverage for all outpatient prescription drugs
through Medicaid managed care organizations for as long as any
medical assistance service described in 42 U.S.C. § 1396d(a)
(relating to definitions) is delivered by a Medicaid managed
care organization.

Section 6. Safety net prescription drug distribution and access
protections.
(a) Distribution and access.--A manufacturer, an agent or
affiliate of a manufacturer or a third-party logistics provider
may not, directly or indirectly, take any of the following
actions unless the acquisition or receipt of the 340B drug is
prohibited by Federal law:
(1) Deny, restrict, prohibit, delay or otherwise
interfere with the acquisition of a 340B drug by, or delivery
of a 340B drug to, a covered entity or contract pharmacy.
(2) Place a condition on the acquisition or receipt of a
340B drug by a covered entity or contract pharmacy.
(b) Claims and utilization data.--A manufacturer or an agent
or affiliate of a manufacturer may not, directly or indirectly,
require a covered entity to submit claims or utilization data as
a condition for allowing the acquisition of a 340B drug by, or
delivery of a 340B drug to, the covered entity unless submission
of the claims or utilization data is required by Federal law.

Section 7. Enforcement, penalties and remedies.
(a) Insurance Department.--The Insurance Department shall
investigate complaints alleging violations of sections 3 and
5(a) and enforce those provisions. In administering this
subsection, the Insurance Department may:
(1) Examine or audit the books and records of a pharmacy
benefits manager, covered insurer or related party to
determine compliance with this act.
(2) Issue subpoenas as necessary to carry out
enforcement and oversight under this act.
(b) Section 6 enforcement.--
(1) A violation of section 6 shall constitute an
unlawful practice within the meaning of the act of December
17, 1968 (P.L.1224, No.387), known as the Unfair Trade
Practices and Consumer Protection Law. The Office of Attorney
General shall have jurisdiction, consistent with the Unfair
Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law, to enforce
section 6 and may promulgate regulations to implement section
6.
(2) The department may promulgate regulations consistent
with section 6. The department may investigate a complaint
alleging a violation of section 6 by an individual or entity
licensed by or registered with the department, impose
discipline on the individual or entity, suspend or revoke the
license or registration of the individual or entity and issue
subpoenas as necessary to administer enforcement and
oversight under this act.
(c) Civil penalties.--
(1) Upon written notice, the Insurance Department shall
impose a civil penalty on a pharmacy benefits manager or

the right of the manufacturer or agent or affiliate of the
manufacturer to conduct business in this Commonwealth.
(e) Contracts.--A term of a contract entered into on or
after the effective date of this subsection that violates
section 3 or 5(a) is void.

Section 8. Construction.
(a) Federal law.--This act shall be construed and applied so
that it is not less restrictive than applicable Federal law with
respect to a person or entity regulated under this act. Nothing
in this act shall be construed or applied in a manner that
conflicts with applicable Federal law or related regulations,
including 42 U.S.C. Ch. 7, Subch. XI, Pt. E (relating to price
negotiation program to lower prices for certain high-priced
single source drugs).
(b) Other laws of Commonwealth.--Nothing in this act shall
be construed or applied in a manner that conflicts with another
law of this Commonwealth that is compatible with applicable
Federal law. If this act and the act of November 21, 2016
(P.L.1318, No.169), known as the Pharmacy Benefit Reform Act,
both apply, this act shall be construed to provide greater
protection for covered entities and contract pharmacies.
(c) Limited distribution.--Limited distribution of a drug
required under 21 U.S.C. § 355-1 (relating to risk evaluation
and mitigation strategies) shall not constitute a violation of
this act.
Section 9. Effective date.
This act shall take effect immediately.
