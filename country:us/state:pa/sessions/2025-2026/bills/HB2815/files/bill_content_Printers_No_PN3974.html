AN ACT
Amending Title 75 (Vehicles) of the Pennsylvania Consolidated
Statutes, providing for protections for victims of vehicle
theft; establishing the Victims of Vehicle Theft Protection
Fund; imposing duties on the Department of Transportation;
and imposing penalties.
This act shall be known and may be cited as Raymond Mason's
Law.
The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania
hereby enacts as follows:
Section 1. Chapter 71 of Title 75 of the Pennsylvania
Consolidated Statutes is amended by adding a subchapter to read:

§ 7141. Definitions.
The following words and phrases when used in this subchapter
shall have the meanings given to them in this section unless the
context clearly indicates otherwise:
"Fund." The Victims of Vehicle Theft Protection Fund
established under section 7147 (relating to Victims of Vehicle
Theft Protection Fund).
"Private entity." Any of the following:
(1) A tow truck operator, as defined in section 2 of the
act of July 5, 2012 (P.L.990, No.110), known as the Towing
and Towing Storage Facility Standards Act.
(2) A towing storage facility as defined in section 2 of
the Towing and Towing Storage Facility Standards Act.
(3) A salvor, as defined in section 102 (relating to
definitions).
(4) Any other nonpublic entity that comes into lawful
possession of a stolen vehicle and which may demand a fee or
other compensation as a precondition of release of the
vehicle to the lawful owner or an authorized representative
of the owner.
"Public entity." Any of the following:
(1) Any parking authority or entity established in
accordance with or otherwise subject to the provisions of 53
Pa.C.S. Ch. 55 (relating to parking authorities).
(2) A municipality or municipal authority.
(3) A police department.

§ 7142. Victim protection.
The owner of a stolen vehicle or an authorized representative
of the owner shall present a police report verifying the vehicle
as reported stolen to claim protection under this subchapter.
The owner of a stolen vehicle shall not be required to present
information other than required under this section.
§ 7143. Fines and fees prohibited.
(a) Prohibition.--No victim of any of the following crimes
may be charged a fee, fine or otherwise be held liable for the
costs of vehicle recovery or storage of the vehicle by a private
or public entity that recovers the victim's vehicle:
(1) 18 Pa.C.S. § 3702 (relating to robbery of motor
vehicle).
(2) 18 Pa.C.S. § 3921 (relating to theft by unlawful
taking or disposition), when the property taken is a vehicle.
(3) 18 Pa.C.S. § 3928 (relating to unauthorized use of
automobiles and other vehicles).
(b) Release of vehicle.--Any recovering entity shall
immediately release the vehicle to the lawful owner or an
authorized representative of the owner when presented with a
police report that verifies the vehicle as being reported
stolen. The recovering entity shall only be required to release

§ 7144. Transfer of ownership prohibited.
(a) Sale or transfer limitation.--Stolen vehicles in the
custody of a recovering entity may not be auctioned, sold or
otherwise have ownership transferred to another entity or
individual, except in accordance with subsection (c).
(b) Abandoned vehicle.--A stolen vehicle shall be considered
abandoned if:
(1) the lawful owner of the vehicle has been notified of
the recovery and location of the stolen vehicle, either by
law enforcement or the recovering entity, through certified
mail and successful or attempted telephone contact; and
(2) more than 30 days have passed from the date of
recovery and the stolen vehicle remains unclaimed by the
lawful owner or 60 days have passed from the date the lawful
owner has been notified of the recovery and location of the
vehicle and the vehicle has not been retrieved. If the entity
has a duty to return the vehicle under section 7145 (relating
to duty to return), the vehicle may only be considered not
retrieved if the lawful owner fails to provide a proper
address or location where the vehicle may be lawfully
returned or stored.
(c) Notification.--If a stolen vehicle becomes abandoned
under subsection (b), the recovering entity shall notify the

§ 7145. Duty to return.
A public entity that maintains possession of a stolen vehicle
has a duty to return or arrange for the return of the vehicle to
the lawful owner's place of residence or storage at no cost to
the owner if the owner's documented place of residence is
farther than 30 miles from the actual location of the vehicle.
Nothing in this section shall be construed to prevent the
continued possession of a vehicle by a public entity for
investigatory purposes or to prevent the lawful owner from
retrieving the vehicle.
§ 7146. Penalties.
(a) Imposition.--A private entity that violates:
(1) Section 7143(a) or (c) (relating to fines and fees
prohibited) shall be subject to a fine levied by the
department of not less than $1,000 and not more than $2,000
for a first offense. Each subsequent offense within two years
of the first offense shall be punishable by a fine of not
less than $2,500 and not more than $10,000.
(2) Section 7143(b) shall be subject to a fine levied by
the department of not less than $250 and not more than $750.
Each day the vehicle is unlawfully retained by the private
entity after the first offense shall result in an additional

§ 7147. Victims of Vehicle Theft Protection Fund.
(a) Establishment.--The Victims of Vehicle Theft Protection
Fund is established in the State Treasury. All fines collected
by the department under section 7146 (relating to penalties)
shall be deposited into the fund. Money in the fund is
appropriated to the department on a continuing basis for the
purposes of this subchapter.
(b) Remittance.--A public entity ordered to pay restitution
to a victim under section 7146 shall remit to the department for
deposit into the fund an amount equal to 50% of the restitution
ordered to be paid to the victim. Nothing in this section shall
be construed to mean that 50% of the restitution amount shall be
diverted from the victim to the fund.
(c) Application.--A victim whose protections under this
subchapter have been violated may apply to the department for
compensation from the fund. Disbursements from the fund shall be
for reimbursement of:
(1) fees assessed on and paid by the victim in violation
of section 7143 (relating to fines and fees prohibited); and
(2) the cost of replacing a vehicle if the ownership of
the victim's vehicle was transferred in violation of section
7144(a) (relating to transfer of ownership prohibited).
(d) Limitation.--Reimbursement amounts shall not exceed the
actual amount paid by the victim if applying for compensation
under subsection (c)(1) or 50% of the fair market value of the
vehicle if applying for compensation under subsection (c)(2).
Compensation shall be awarded in order of application as money
in the fund is available.

§ 7148. Contracts with private entities.
No public entity may contract with or continue to contract
with a private entity that has violated the provisions of this
subchapter more than five times in three years. This section
shall:
(1) Serve as legal justification for the termination of
contracts violated after the effective date of this
paragraph.
(2) Override relevant termination provisions that may be
present within a contract entered into or renewed after the
effective date of the contract.
(3) Absolve the public entity of all further obligations
to the private entity that may have been provided for in the
terminated contract.
§ 7149. Authority and duties of department.
(a) Complaints.--The department shall establish a mechanism
to receive and investigate complaints of violations of this
subchapter.
(b) Notification.--The department shall, to the greatest
extent possible, annually inform all public and private entities
of this subchapter.
Section 2. This act shall take effect in 60 days.
