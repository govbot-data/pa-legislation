andWHEREAS, On October 23, 1956, the people of Hungary rose in a courageous national uprising against the communist dictatorship imposed under the influence of the Soviet Union; andWHEREAS, This revolt represented one of the most significant challenges to communist rule during the Cold War, demonstrating the determination of the Hungarian people to live in freedom and with self-governance, national sovereignty, freedom of speech and respect for fundamental human rights; andWHEREAS, The uprising lasted 15 days before Soviet military forces brutally suppressed the revolution, resulting in thousands of deaths and injuries, the imprisonment and execution of political leaders and dissidents and the forced exile of approximately 200,000 Hungarians; and1234567891011121314151617 WHEREAS, The historical record demonstrates that communist regimes across Central and Eastern Europe and the world were responsible for widespread violations of human rights, political repression, forced labor, imprisonment and deaths of millions of people; andWHEREAS, Many Hungarian immigrants settled in this Commonwealth, joining established Hungarian-American communities in cities and towns across this Commonwealth, including Pittsburgh, Bethlehem, McKeesport, Johnstown, Ligonier and the greater Philadelphia region; andWHEREAS, Hungarian immigrants and their descendants made significant contributions to this Commonwealth's economic growth through their work in manufacturing, steel production, coal mining, skilled trades, small business ownership, engineering, medicine, education, scientific research and entrepreneurship; andWHEREAS, These Hungarian Americans strengthened the civic, cultural, religious and economic fabric of Pennsylvania while preserving their heritage and passing on the values of hard work, faith, family and freedom;
