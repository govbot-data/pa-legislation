AN ACT
Providing for hazardous tree removal and remediation,
disposition of wood material and sidewalk repair or
replacement within municipalities and for responsibilities of
municipalities and property owners.
The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania
hereby enacts as follows:
Section 1. Short title.
This act shall be known and may be cited as the Public Right-
of-Way Safety Act.
Section 2. Legislative findings.
The General Assembly finds and declares as follows:
(1) Trees that are diseased, dying or structurally
unstable and located near public roads or sidewalks present a
serious threat to public health and safety.
(2) Current State law lacks uniform and consistent
authority across all classes of municipalities to address
hazardous trees that originate on private property but
endanger the public right-of-way.
(3) Property owners bear responsibility for maintaining

safe conditions on and around their land.
(4) However, when a tree is planted by a municipality or
is required by a municipality to be planted within a shade
tree area pursuant to an ordinance, policy or condition of
land use approval, it is inequitable to impose the full cost
of removal or remediation on the property owner.
(5) Sidewalk damage caused by trees located within a
shade tree area and planted by the municipality or required
by the municipality to be planted within the shade tree area
creates hazardous conditions for pedestrians and may place an
undue financial burden on adjacent property owners if cost
responsibilities are not clearly defined.
(6) A uniform, Statewide policy is necessary to equip
municipalities with the authority to protect public safety
while ensuring fair treatment of property owners.
Section 3. Definitions.
The following words and phrases when used in this act shall
have the meanings given to them in this section unless the
context clearly indicates otherwise:
"Hazardous tree." A tree that, because of disease,
structural condition or location, poses an unreasonable risk to
public safety or unreasonably interferes with the public's use
of a public right-of-way.
"Municipality." Any of the following:
(1) A city of the third class.
(2) A borough or incorporated town.
(3) A township of the first class.
(4) A township of the second class.
(5) A municipality operating under a home rule charter
or optional plan of government.

"Property owner." A person or legal entity that holds legal
title to real property located within a municipality.
"Public right-of-way." The area within the legally
established right-of-way of a publicly owned or maintained
street, highway or alley, including a sidewalk or a similar
corridor intended for public travel and located adjacent to and
functionally integrated with the roadway. The term does not
include trails, paths or sidewalks located entirely within a
public park or recreational area that are not adjacent to a
public street or highway.
"Remediation." Measures short of full removal taken to
eliminate or mitigate a hazardous tree condition, including
pruning, trimming, bracing or other structural corrective action
necessary to protect public safety or a public right-of-way if
the hazardous condition is corrected.
"Removal." The complete cutting down and extraction of a
hazardous tree from the tree's site, including felling of the
trunk and grinding or extraction of the stump to or below ground
level unless a local ordinance allows the stump to remain under
specified conditions.
"Shade tree area." The portion of a public right-of-way
located between the curb or curb line of a public roadway and an
adjacent sidewalk, or within a tree well, tree pit or other
designated planting area incorporated into or immediately
adjacent to the sidewalk, that is intended for the planting or
maintenance of shade trees or other vegetation.
"Wood material." Logs, cordwood, branch wood, wood chips,
sawdust, brush or other byproducts resulting from the removal or
remediation of a tree.

(a) Ordinance authorization.--A municipality may adopt an
ordinance requiring a property owner to remove or remediate a
hazardous tree located on the property if the tree poses a risk
to public safety or obstructs a public right-of-way.
(b) Ordinance requirements.--An ordinance adopted under
subsection (a) shall include all of the following:
(1) Written notice to the property owner, sent by
certified mail with return receipt requested, identifying the
hazardous condition and directing the property owner to
remove or remediate the tree and advising the property owner
of the consequences of failure to comply under paragraph (3).
(2) A compliance period for the property owner of not
fewer than 30 days after the date of notice under paragraph
(1), unless the municipality determines that an imminent
hazard requires earlier intervention.
(3) Authority for the municipality to enter the property
and remove or remediate the hazardous tree if the property
owner fails to comply within the time period specified under
paragraph (2). A property owner who fails to comply shall
forfeit any claim to the resulting wood material, unless
otherwise agreed in writing by the municipality and the
property owner. The municipality may proceed with disposition
of the wood material in accordance with subsection (c).
(4) Authority for the municipality to recover the actual
costs of tree removal, remediation or the disposition of wood
material under subsection (c), which shall be collected as a
municipal lien against the property in the same manner as
other municipal claims.
(c) Disposition of wood material.--Except as provided under
subsection (d), the following shall apply:

Section 5. Sidewalk repair or replacement.
(a) Ordinance authorization.--A municipality may adopt an
ordinance establishing responsibility for the repair or
replacement of a sidewalk within a public right-of-way that is
damaged by a tree.
(b) Allocation of responsibility.--An ordinance adopted
under subsection (a) shall provide the following:
(1) The municipality shall be responsible for the cost
of repair or replacement of a sidewalk damaged by a tree if:
(i) the tree was planted by the municipality; or
(ii) the tree was required by the municipality to be
planted within a shade tree area pursuant to an
ordinance, policy or condition of land use approval.
(2) Except as provided under paragraph (1), the property
owner shall be responsible for the cost of repair or
replacement of a sidewalk damaged by:
(i) a tree located on the property; or
(ii) a tree located within a shade tree area
adjacent to the property that was not planted by the
municipality or required by the municipality to be
planted within the shade tree area.
(c) Property owner enforcement.--An ordinance adopted under
subsection (a) shall, with respect to a property owner's
responsibility under subsection (b)(2), include all of the
following:

(2) A compliance period for the property owner of not

municipal lien against the property in the same manner as
other municipal claims.
Section 6. Construction.
Nothing in this act shall be construed to limit or impair the
authority of municipalities under any other provision of law to
regulate vegetation, abate nuisances or maintain public
infrastructure.
Section 7. Effective date.
This act shall take effect in 60 days.
