2025-2026 Regular Session SB 1477 PN 1995 Bill Text (HTM) See other bills under thesame topic PRINTER'S NO. 1995 THE GENERAL ASSEMBLY OF PENNSYLVANIASENATE BILL No.1477 Session of 2026 INTRODUCED BY BOSCOLA, VOGEL, STEFANO AND MILLER, SEPTEMBER 30, 2026 REFERRED TO LOCAL GOVERNMENT, SEPTEMBER 30, 2026 AN ACTProviding for hazardous tree removal and remediation, disposition of wood material and sidewalk repair or replacement within municipalities and for responsibilities of municipalities and property owners.The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania hereby enacts as follows:Section 1. Short title.This act shall be known and may be cited as the Public Right-of-Way Safety Act.Section 2. Legislative findings.The General Assembly finds and declares as follows:(1) Trees that are diseased, dying or structurally unstable and located near public roads or sidewalks present a serious threat to public health and safety.(2) Current State law lacks uniform and consistent authority across all classes of municipalities to address hazardous trees that originate on private property but endanger the public right-of-way.(3) Property owners bear responsibility for maintaining 12345678910111213141516171819 safe conditions on and around their land.(4) However, when a tree is planted by a municipality or is required by a municipality to be planted within a shade tree area pursuant to an ordinance, policy or condition of land use approval, it is inequitable to impose the full cost of removal or remediation on the property owner.(5) Sidewalk damage caused by trees located within a shade tree area and planted by the municipality or required by the municipality to be planted within the shade tree area creates hazardous conditions for pedestrians and may place an undue financial burden on adjacent property owners if cost responsibilities are not clearly defined.(6) A uniform, Statewide policy is necessary to equip municipalities with the authority to protect public safety while ensuring fair treatment of property owners.Section 3. Definitions.The following words and phrases when used in this act shall have the meanings given to them in this section unless the context clearly indicates otherwise:"Hazardous tree." A tree that, because of disease, structural condition or location, poses an unreasonable risk to public safety or unreasonably interferes with the public's use of a public right-of-way."Municipality." Any of the following:(1) A city of the third class.(2) A borough or incorporated town.(3) A township of the first class.(4) A township of the second class.(5) A municipality operating under a home rule charter or optional plan of government.20260SB1477PN1995 - 2 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 "Property owner." A person or legal entity that holds legal title to real property located within a municipality."Public right-of-way." The area within the legally established right-of-way of a publicly owned or maintained street, highway or alley, including a sidewalk or a similar corridor intended for public travel and located adjacent to and functionally integrated with the roadway. The term does not include trails, paths or sidewalks located entirely within a public park or recreational area that are not adjacent to a public street or highway."Remediation." Measures short of full removal taken to eliminate or mitigate a hazardous tree condition, including pruning, trimming, bracing or other structural corrective action necessary to protect public safety or a public right-of-way if the hazardous condition is corrected."Removal." The complete cutting down and extraction of a hazardous tree from the tree's site, including felling of the trunk and grinding or extraction of the stump to or below ground level unless a local ordinance allows the stump to remain under specified conditions."Shade tree area." The portion of a public right-of-way located between the curb or curb line of a public roadway and an adjacent sidewalk, or within a tree well, tree pit or other designated planting area incorporated into or immediately adjacent to the sidewalk, that is intended for the planting or maintenance of shade trees or other vegetation."Wood material." Logs, cordwood, branch wood, wood chips, sawdust, brush or other byproducts resulting from the removal or remediation of a tree.Section 4. Hazardous tree removal and remediation.20260SB1477PN1995 - 3 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 (a) Ordinance authorization.--A municipality may adopt an ordinance requiring a property owner to remove or remediate a hazardous tree located on the property if the tree poses a risk to public safety or obstructs a public right-of-way.(b) Ordinance requirements.--An ordinance adopted under subsection (a) shall include all of the following:(1) Written notice to the property owner, sent by certified mail with return receipt requested, identifying the hazardous condition and directing the property owner to remove or remediate the tree and advising the property owner of the consequences of failure to comply under paragraph (3). (2) A compliance period for the property owner of not fewer than 30 days after the date of notice under paragraph (1), unless the municipality determines that an imminent hazard requires earlier intervention.(3) Authority for the municipality to enter the property and remove or remediate the hazardous tree if the property owner fails to comply within the time period specified under paragraph (2). A property owner who fails to comply shall forfeit any claim to the resulting wood material, unless otherwise agreed in writing by the municipality and the property owner. The municipality may proceed with disposition of the wood material in accordance with subsection (c).(4) Authority for the municipality to recover the actual costs of tree removal, remediation or the disposition of wood material under subsection (c), which shall be collected as a municipal lien against the property in the same manner as other municipal claims.(c) Disposition of wood material.--Except as provided under subsection (d), the following shall apply:20260SB1477PN1995 - 4 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 (1) If the property owner removes or remediates the hazardous tree, all resulting wood material shall remain the property of the property owner. The property owner shall remove the wood material from the public right-of-way not later than 10 days following the date of removal or remediation. Sidewalk repair or replacement.(a) Ordinance authorization.--A municipality may adopt an ordinance establishing responsibility for the repair or replacement of a sidewalk within a public right-of-way that is damaged by a tree.(b) Allocation of responsibility.--An ordinance adopted under subsection (a) shall provide the following:(1) The municipality shall be responsible for the cost of repair or replacement of a sidewalk damaged by a tree if:(i) the tree was planted by the municipality; or(ii) the tree was required by the municipality to be planted within a shade tree area pursuant to an ordinance, policy or condition of land use approval.(2) Except as provided under paragraph (1), the property owner shall be responsible for the cost of repair or replacement of a sidewalk damaged by:(i) a tree located on the property; or(ii) a tree located within a shade tree area adjacent to the property that was not planted by the municipality or required by the municipality to be planted within the shade tree area.(c) Property owner enforcement.--An ordinance adopted under subsection (a) shall, with respect to a property owner's responsibility under subsection (b)(2), include all of the following:20260SB1477PN1995 - 6 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 (1) Written notice to the property owner, sent by certified mail with return receipt requested, identifying the damaged sidewalk condition and directing the property owner to repair or replace the affected sidewalk.(2) A compliance period for the property owner of not fewer than 30 days after the date of notice under paragraph (1), unless the municipality determines that an imminent hazard requires earlier intervention.(3) Authority for the municipality to repair or replace the damaged sidewalk if the property owner fails to act within the time period specified under paragraph (2), including authority to enter the property if reasonably necessary to perform the repair or replacement.(4) Authority for the municipality to recover the actual costs of repair or replacement, which shall be collected as a municipal lien against the property in the same manner as other municipal claims.Section 6. Construction.Nothing in this act shall be construed to limit or impair the authority of municipalities under any other provision of law to regulate vegetation, abate nuisances or maintain public infrastructure.Section 7. Effective date.This act shall take effect in 60 days.20260SB1477PN1995 - 7 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324
