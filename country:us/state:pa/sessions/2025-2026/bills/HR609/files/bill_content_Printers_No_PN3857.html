A RESOLUTION
Designating the month of November 2026 as "Prematurity Awareness
Month" and November 17, 2026, as "Prematurity Awareness Day"
in Pennsylvania.
WHEREAS, Pregnancies are typically considered full term when
childbirth occurs at 39 weeks, although birth occurring between
37 weeks and 39 weeks is often referred to as early term; and
WHEREAS, Factors associated with higher preterm birth rates
include having a prior preterm birth, less access to prenatal
care services and preexisting health conditions; and
WHEREAS, Twin, triplet or other multiple pregnancies and the
use of assistive reproductive technology have also been
associated with an increased risk of preterm birth; and
WHEREAS, Higher rates of preterm births have been identified
among Black, Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander and American
Indian or Alaska Native women; and
WHEREAS, Premature infants, especially those born extremely
premature, may require specialized medical care and weeks or
months of hospitalization in a Neonatal Intensive Care Unit; and

WHEREAS, Although medical advancements have helped improve
outcomes and survival rates for premature infants, premature
birth remains among the leading causes of death for newborn
infants; and
WHEREAS, Infants born prematurely may also face adverse
health conditions over the course of their lives, including
chronic respiratory problems, cerebral palsy, a higher chance of
having a learning, behavioral or intellectual disability, vision
and hearing loss, feeding and digestive problems, enamel or
other dental defects, infections and cardiovascular diseases
when compared to full-term infants; and
WHEREAS, Infants born prematurely have an increased risk of
sudden infant death syndrome; and
WHEREAS, Experiencing a preterm birth also increases the risk
of anxiety, postpartum depression, post-traumatic stress
disorder and struggles with bonding with the infant for the
mother and family; and
WHEREAS, Approximately 1 in 10 infants are born prematurely
in the United States every year; and
WHEREAS, In total, 12,417 infants were born prematurely in
Pennsylvania in 2024; and
WHEREAS, This Commonwealth ranked 17 out of 50 states,
Washington, DC, and Puerto Rico, in a report from the March of
Dimes, with a 2024 preterm birth rate of 9.8%; and
WHEREAS, The preterm birth rate of 9.8% in 2024 represented
an increase in the number of preterm births from 2023 to 2024,
and Pennsylvania received a preterm birth grade of "C" on its
report card from the March of Dimes; and
WHEREAS, The March of Dimes has declared November as
"Prematurity Awareness Month" and November 15 as "World
