WILLIAMS, NEILSON, COOPER, BOYD AND CEPEDA-FREYTIZ, SEPTEMBER 1, 2026 REFERRED TO COMMITTEE ON CHILDREN AND YOUTH, SEPTEMBER 2, 2026 A RESOLUTIONDesignating the month of November 2026 as "Prematurity Awareness Month" and November 17, 2026, as "Prematurity Awareness Day" in Pennsylvania.WHEREAS, Pregnancies are typically considered full term when childbirth occurs at 39 weeks, although birth occurring between 37 weeks and 39 weeks is often referred to as early term; andWHEREAS, Factors associated with higher preterm birth rates include having a prior preterm birth, less access to prenatal care services and preexisting health conditions; andWHEREAS, Twin, triplet or other multiple pregnancies and the use of assistive reproductive technology have also been associated with an increased risk of preterm birth; andWHEREAS, Higher rates of preterm births have been identified among Black, Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander and American Indian or Alaska Native women; andWHEREAS, Premature infants, especially those born extremely premature, may require specialized medical care and weeks or months of hospitalization in a Neonatal Intensive Care Unit; and123456789101112131415161718 WHEREAS, Although medical advancements have helped improve outcomes and survival rates for premature infants, premature birth remains among the leading causes of death for newborn infants; andWHEREAS, Infants born prematurely may also face adverse health conditions over the course of their lives, including chronic respiratory problems, cerebral palsy, a higher chance of having a learning, behavioral or intellectual disability, vision and hearing loss, feeding and digestive problems, enamel or other dental defects, infections and cardiovascular diseases when compared to full-term infants; andWHEREAS, Infants born prematurely have an increased risk of sudden infant death syndrome; andWHEREAS, Experiencing a preterm birth also increases the risk of anxiety, postpartum depression, post-traumatic stress disorder and struggles with bonding with the infant for the mother and family; andWHEREAS, Approximately 1 in 10 infants are born prematurely in the United States every year; andWHEREAS, In total, 12,417 infants were born prematurely in Pennsylvania in 2024; andWHEREAS, This Commonwealth ranked 17 out of 50 states, Washington, DC, and Puerto Rico, in a report from the March of Dimes, with a 2024 preterm birth rate of 9.8%; andWHEREAS, The preterm birth rate of 9.8% in 2024 represented an increase in the number of preterm births from 2023 to 2024, and Pennsylvania received a preterm birth grade of "C" on its report card from the March of Dimes;
