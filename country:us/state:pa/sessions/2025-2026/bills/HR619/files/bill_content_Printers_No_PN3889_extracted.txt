andWHEREAS, Born January 19, 1946, in Locust Ridge, Tennessee, Dolly Parton grew up in the Appalachian Mountains as 1 of 12 children in a family that struggled financially; andWHEREAS, Dolly Parton, throughout her songwriting and career, drew from her working-class upbringing and Appalachian roots; andWHEREAS, Dolly Parton became one of the most celebrated songwriters in American music, writing enduring songs such as "Jolene," "Coat of Many Colors," "9 to 5" and "I Will Always 12345678910111213141516 Love You"; andWHEREAS, Dolly Parton's songwriting brought stories of family, poverty, work, love, loss, resilience and aspiration to audiences around the world, transforming personal experiences and working-class life into music that resonated across generations; andWHEREAS, Dolly Parton helped expand opportunities for women in country music and established herself as an artist with extraordinary creative and business independence, including the founding of her own record label; andWHEREAS, Dolly Parton crossed artistic boundaries throughout her career, bringing her distinctive voice and songwriting to country, pop, bluegrass, gospel, rock, film, television and theater; andWHEREAS, Dolly Parton expanded her artistic work into film, starring in such celebrated films as "9 to 5," "The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas" and "Steel Magnolias," while earning Academy Award nominations for songs she wrote for film; andWHEREAS, Dolly Parton wrote the music and lyrics for the Broadway musical "9 to 5," bringing her songwriting and storytelling to the American theater and earning a Tony Award nomination for Best Original Score; andWHEREAS, Dolly Parton was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1999, the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2001 and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2022, reflecting the breadth and lasting influence of her work across musical genres; andWHEREAS, Dolly Parton received the National Medal of Arts in 2005, the highest award given to artists by the Federal Government, and the Kennedy Center Honors in 2006 in recognition of her contributions to American culture;
