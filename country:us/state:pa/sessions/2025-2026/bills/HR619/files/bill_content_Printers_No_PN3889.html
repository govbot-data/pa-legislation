A RESOLUTION
Designating September 25, 2026, as "Dolly Parton Day" in
Pennsylvania.
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton was a singer, songwriter, musician,
producer, actress, playwright, author, businesswoman and
philanthropist, whose seven-decade career made her one of the
most influential figures in American music and popular culture;
and
WHEREAS, Born January 19, 1946, in Locust Ridge, Tennessee,
Dolly Parton grew up in the Appalachian Mountains as 1 of 12
children in a family that struggled financially; and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton, throughout her songwriting and career,
drew from her working-class upbringing and Appalachian roots;
and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton became one of the most celebrated
songwriters in American music, writing enduring songs such as
"Jolene," "Coat of Many Colors," "9 to 5" and "I Will Always

WHEREAS, Dolly Parton's songwriting brought stories of
family, poverty, work, love, loss, resilience and aspiration to
audiences around the world, transforming personal experiences
and working-class life into music that resonated across
generations; and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton helped expand opportunities for women
in country music and established herself as an artist with
extraordinary creative and business independence, including the
founding of her own record label; and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton crossed artistic boundaries throughout
her career, bringing her distinctive voice and songwriting to
country, pop, bluegrass, gospel, rock, film, television and
theater; and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton expanded her artistic work into film,
starring in such celebrated films as "9 to 5," "The Best Little
Whorehouse in Texas" and "Steel Magnolias," while earning
Academy Award nominations for songs she wrote for film; and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton wrote the music and lyrics for the
Broadway musical "9 to 5," bringing her songwriting and
storytelling to the American theater and earning a Tony Award
nomination for Best Original Score; and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton was inducted into the Country Music
Hall of Fame in 1999, the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2001 and
the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2022, reflecting the breadth
and lasting influence of her work across musical genres; and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton received the National Medal of Arts in
2005, the highest award given to artists by the Federal
Government, and the Kennedy Center Honors in 2006 in recognition
of her contributions to American culture; and

WHEREAS, Dolly Parton demonstrated the power of the arts to
reach people across differences in geography, age, class and
musical tradition, using songwriting and storytelling to bring
Appalachian and working-class experiences to audiences around
the world; and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton founded the Dollywood Foundation in
1988 and created Dolly Parton's Imagination Library in 1995,
providing young children with free, high-quality books and
helping to make access to reading and literature a part of
childhood for millions of families; and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton's Imagination Library has become a
model for how artists can use their influence to expand access
to literature and the arts, with local partners helping to bring
free books to children in communities across this Commonwealth,
including through the West Philadelphia Alliance for Children;
and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton's Imagination Library has distributed
more than 300 million books to children since its inception,
creating a lasting legacy of literacy and learning in
communities throughout the United States and around the world;
and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton repeatedly used her success and
resources to support communities in times of crisis, including
through the My People Fund, which provided more than $13 million
in direct recovery funds to families affected by the 2016
wildfires in Sevier County, Tennessee; and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton supported pediatric health and medical
research through major gifts to Vanderbilt University Medical
Center, including a $1 million donation in 2020 to support
COVID-19 research that contributed to work associated with the

WHEREAS, Following Hurricane Helene in 2024, Dolly Parton
committed $1 million to the Mountain Ways Foundation for

companies for a combined $2 million contribution; and
WHEREAS, Dolly Parton was awarded the Carnegie Medal of

WHEREAS, Dolly Parton's artistic legacy continues through the
musicians who perform her songs, the writers and performers
influenced by her work and the children discovering the joy of
reading through the Imagination Library, including children and
families throughout this Commonwealth; therefore be it
RESOLVED, That the House of Representatives designate
September 25, 2026, as "Dolly Parton Day" in Pennsylvania.
