SOF Frank J. McCloskey Memorial Bridge.The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania hereby enacts as follows:Section 1. Sgt. SOF Frank J. McCloskey Memorial Bridge.(a) Findings.--The General Assembly finds and declares as follows:(1) Francis J. McCloskey, Jr., was born August 1, 1948.(2) A graduate of United High School, he enlisted in the United States Army on June 3, 1966. Mr. McCloskey trained at Fort Bragg and Fort Benning.(3) Upon completion of his training, Mr. McCloskey deployed with the 502nd Battalion of the 101st Airborne Division in Vietnam for his first tour.(4) Mr. McCloskey was wounded in action during his first tour and was awarded the Purple Heart, Combat Infantryman Badge and Bronze Star with Valor.1234567891011121314151617181920 (5) Mr. McCloskey volunteered for Special Forces duty after his first tour, earned his Green Beret and served with the Military Assistance Command, Vietnam - Studies and Observations Group, commanding his own recon team in 1968. Mr. McCloskey was awarded his second Bronze Star Medal with Valor after undertaking heroic measures moving his team to safety during an overwhelming enemy attack.(6) Mr. McCloskey was transferred to the 11th Special Forces Group (Reserves) in Ft. Mead, Maryland, in 1969 and was sworn in as a police officer with the Metropolitan Police District of Columbia (MPDC).(7) Mr. McCloskey was later promoted to detective at MPDC, where he was part of the response to the attempted assassination of President Ronald Reagan in March 1981.(8) Mr. McCloskey was recruited by the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) where he performed clandestine operations in the Latin American Division and was trained as a helicopter pilot in 1981.(9) CIA Director William Casey awarded Mr. McCloskey the Intelligence Star for Valor for actions taken to save another during a violent guerrilla assault. Mr. McCloskey was also the only non-Guatemalan military member to receive a Battle Cross for Valor.(10) Mr. McCloskey retired from government service in 1995.(11) Mr. McCloskey was selected to be Chief of Police for the Chitimacha Tribal Police Department in Louisiana, where he assisted with prosecutions navigating Tribal Law and Napoleonic Code in 1996 and later was hired as a detective in the St. Mary Parish Sheriff's Office.
