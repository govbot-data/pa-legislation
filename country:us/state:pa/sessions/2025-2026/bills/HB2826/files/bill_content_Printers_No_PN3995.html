§ 10101. Scope of part.
This part relates to firefighter cancer screening.
§ 10102. Definitions.
Subject to additional definitions contained in subsequent
provisions of this part which are applicable to specific
provisions of this part, the following words and phrases when
used in this part shall have the meanings given to them in this
section unless the context clearly indicates otherwise:
"Airport fire company." A fire company that meets all of the
following:
(1) Is associated with an airport.
(2) Has a mutual aid agreement with a neighboring
municipality.
(3) Has responded to at least 15 fire or rescue
emergency calls annually to neighboring fire companies as
provided in documentation requested.
"Cancer screening." Testing, imaging, evaluation or
examination to detect the presence of cancer or abnormal cells
that may become cancer.
"Community health district." One of the six regional
divisions of this Commonwealth as identified by the Department
of Health under section 2105 of the act of April 9, 1929
(P.L.177, No.175), known as The Administrative Code of 1929.
"Eligible firefighter." A firefighter that is eligible under
section 10302(b) (relating to firefighter cancer screening
program).
"Fire company." A volunteer fire company, municipal fire
company or combined volunteer and municipal fire company located
in this Commonwealth. The term includes an airport fire company.

§ 10302. Firefighter cancer screening program.
(a) Establishment.--To the extent money is available in the
fund, the department shall establish and administer a program to
make available cancer screenings to eligible firefighters as
provided under this part.
(b) Eligibility.--The department shall determine a
firefighter's eligibility for cancer screenings as provided by
the program. A firefighter shall be eligible if:
(1) The firefighter meets the following requirements:
(i) the firefighter has served 12 or more months in
continuous firefighting duties; and
(ii) the firefighter has actively responded to at
least 15 fire or rescue emergencies during the previous
calendar year, as determined and verified by the
firefighter's fire company.
(2) The firefighter is covered by section 301(f) of the
act of June 2, 1915 (P.L.736, No.338), known as the Workers'
Compensation Act.
(c) Screening locations.--Subject to money being available
in the fund, cancer screenings shall be provided at least twice
per year in each community health district.
(d) Screening availability.--Subject to money being
available in the fund, once every three years, an eligible
firefighter may obtain cancer screenings from a program
screening entity.

(e) Option.--An eligible firefighter may decline to obtain a

(f) Screening types.--Subject to available funds, an

(h) Additional cancer screenings.--
(1) Nothing in this chapter shall prohibit a firefighter
from paying out-of-pocket for additional cancer screenings
provided by the program screening entity.
(2) The program screening entity shall inform the
firefighter that the purchase of additional cancer screenings
is not a condition of receiving cancer screenings under this
part.
(i) Data.--The program shall collect the following data,
which may be shared with the Department of Health and the Office
of the Fire Commissioner:
(1) Demographic information, if voluntarily provided by
the firefighter, including the number of years the individual
has been serving as a firefighter.
(2) The number of positive cancer screenings, delineated
by cancer diagnosis and community service area.
(j) Reports.--By June 1 of the second year of the program
and each June 1 thereafter, in which the program operates, the
department shall prepare and submit a report to the General
Assembly that provides:
(1) The number of cancer screenings provided in each
year of program operation and cumulatively since the
inception of the program for each community health district.
(2) An accounting of the fund balance, including any
amounts generated by investment income.
(3) Recommendations, if any, as to the types and
frequency of cancer screenings to be provided under the
program.

§ 10303. Contracting and procurement.
(a) Procurement.--
(1) The department may contract with persons and
entities as deemed necessary in order to administer the
program, including an entity to determine eligibility and one
or more program screening entities.
(2) The department shall be exempt from the provisions
of 62 Pa.C.S. Pts. I (relating to Commonwealth Procurement
Code) and II (relating to general procurement provisions) for
the purposes of this subsection.
(b) Preference.--An entity that can screen for the following
conditions shall be preferred:
(1) A multicancer blood test that detects signals from
as many cancer types as possible, including those that are
commonly unscreened.
(2) An ultrasound diagnostic that screens, at minimum:
(i) Abdominal aorta.
(ii) Thyroid.
(iii) Liver.
(iv) Gallbladder.
(v) Spleen.
(vi) Bladder.
(vii) Kidney.
(viii) Testicles.
(ix) Exterior pelvis for females.
(3) An echocardiogram and a carotid doppler.

§ 10304. Regulations and guidance.
The department may promulgate regulations and issue guidance
as necessary and appropriate to implement this part.
§ 10305. Entitlement.
Nothing in this part shall constitute an entitlement derived
from the Commonwealth or a claim on any money of the
Commonwealth.
§ 10306. Confidentiality.
(a) Requirements.--Except as provided in this part, all
working papers, recorded information, documents and copies of
working papers, recorded information and documents produced by,
obtained by or disclosed to the department for fund or program
purposes or any other person in the course of the exercise of
the department's powers and duties relating to the fund or
program under this part:
(1) shall be confidential;
(2) shall not be subject to subpoena;
(3) shall be exempt from access under the act of
February 14, 2008 (P.L.6, No.3), known as the Right-to-Know
Law;
(4) shall not be subject to discovery or admissible in
evidence in any private civil action; and
(5) may not be made public by the program, fund or any
other person.

§ 10307. Outreach.
To the extent funds are made available and in consultation
with the Office of Fire Commissioner and the Department of
Health, the department shall conduct educational outreach and
create materials regarding the availability and benefits of
cancer screenings under the program to the following:
(1) The fire chief and president of each volunteer fire
company.
(2) The chief executive of each municipality in which a
municipal fire company is located.
(3) The head of each combined volunteer and municipal
fire company and each airport fire company.
