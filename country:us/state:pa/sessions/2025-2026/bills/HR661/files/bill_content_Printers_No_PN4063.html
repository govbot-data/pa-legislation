A RESOLUTION
Directing the Joint State Government Commission to conduct a
study on the feasibility of implementing automated transit
systems in this Commonwealth and issue a report.
WHEREAS, Public transportation plays a vital role in the
lives of many Pennsylvanians; and
WHEREAS, The importance of public transportation warrants
further research into methods of expanding, modernizing and
improving public transportation systems; and
WHEREAS, An automated transit system (ATS) is an organized
network of coordinated passenger vehicles that can operate
without an onboard driver, in part through the use of dedicated
infrastructure that separates the vehicles from other modes of
transportation; and
WHEREAS, The concept of ATS has existed since the 1960s and
has been implemented at varying scales throughout the world; and
WHEREAS, ATS has been used to accommodate passenger demand
that exceeds the capacity of other modes of transportation and
provide service according to a consistent, automated schedule;

WHEREAS, An ATS may offer a cost advantage over a subway
system, particularly in a smaller city or suburban community,
because an ATS may require less extensive infrastructure; and
WHEREAS, The International Electrotechnical Commission
classifies levels of urban rail automation from GoA0 through
GoA4 based on the extent to which automated systems perform
functions relating to starting and stopping a train, closing
doors, detecting obstacles and collisions, operating during a
disruption and evacuating passengers; and
WHEREAS, An ATS operating at GoA4 is fully automated, does
not require permanent staff on board during normal operation and
allows personnel to monitor operations and manage emergencies
remotely; and
WHEREAS, Well-known examples of ATS operating at GoA4 include
the Vancouver SkyTrain, Honolulu Skyline, Dubai Metro, Kobe New
Transit Port Island Line, Copenhagen Metro and Line 4 of the São
Paulo Metro; and
WHEREAS, Pittsburgh International Airport operated an
automated underground people mover until 2025; and
WHEREAS, Implementing an ATS could expand access to public
transportation within a metropolitan area and generate economic
benefits for surrounding suburban communities; and
WHEREAS, This Commonwealth could benefit from examining the
feasibility of expanding its public transportation
infrastructure to include an ATS; therefore be it
RESOLVED, That the House of Representatives direct the Joint
State Government Commission to conduct a study on the
feasibility of implementing automated transit systems in this
Commonwealth and issue a report; and be it further

RESOLVED, That the study include, at a minimum, all of the
following:
(1) The most viable means of implementing an automated
transit system, including potential locations and the
authority under which the system would operate.
(2) The estimated cost of constructing an automated
transit system, including costs associated with all of the
following:
(i) Vehicles.
(ii) Guideway or track infrastructure.
(iii) Integration with municipalities and existing
public transportation stops.
(iv) Labor.
(v) The exercise of eminent domain and relocation.
(vi) Geographic barriers.
(vii) Other factors the Joint State Government
Commission deems necessary.
(3) The estimated cost of maintaining an automated
transit system during operation and appropriate funding
sources, including potential revenue from passenger fares.
(4) Federal and State laws applicable to funding the
construction and operation of transportation systems that do
not employ onboard staff.
(5) An examination of existing automated transit systems
and identification of contacts with expertise in the
construction, maintenance and administration of those
systems.
(6) Potential conflicts between an automated transit
system and existing transportation systems and roadways,
including whether an elevated guideway would be necessary;
