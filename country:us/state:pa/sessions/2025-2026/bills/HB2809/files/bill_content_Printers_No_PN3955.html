AN ACT
Amending Title 18 (Crimes and Offenses) of the Pennsylvania
Consolidated Statutes, in trade and commerce, providing for
the offense of unlicensed sale of liquor; and making a
repeal.
The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania
hereby enacts as follows:
Section 1. Title 18 of the Pennsylvania Consolidated
Statutes is amended by adding a section to read:
§ 7333. Unlicensed sale of liquor.
(a) Penalties.--A person who is convicted of violating
section 491(1) of the Liquor Code shall be sentenced as follows:
(1) For a first offense, to pay a fine of not less than
$1,000 nor more than $5,000.
(2) For a second offense committed within five years
after the date of conviction for a prior violation, to:
(i) pay a fine of not less than $5,000 nor more than
$10,000; and
(ii) a term of imprisonment that shall be fixed by
the court at not more than one year.

(3) For a third or subsequent offense committed within
five years after the date of conviction for a prior
violation, to:
(i) pay a fine of not less than $10,000 nor more
than $25,000; and
(ii) a term of imprisonment that shall be fixed by
the court at not more than two years.
(b) Organized unlawful alcohol sales operation.--A violation
of section 491(1) of the Liquor Code constitutes a misdemeanor
of the first degree if the Commonwealth proves beyond a
reasonable doubt that the defendant knowingly and intentionally
operated an organized unlawful liquor sales operation for
commercial profit.
(c) Aggravating circumstances.--In determining penalties or
enforcement priorities, aggravating circumstances may include,
in order of seriousness:
(1) Occurrence of unlawful alcohol activity in
connection with violent criminal conduct or other serious
offenses.
(2) Repeated police responses to the same location
indicating ongoing unlawful operations.
(3) Violations involving occupancy limits or fire and
safety code violations.
(d) Limited concurrent municipal enforcement relating to
unlawful liquor sales activity.--
(1) A law enforcement officer authorized by law to
exercise municipal police powers and acting within the scope
of otherwise lawful duties may enforce section 491(1) of the
Liquor Code and applicable provisions of this title relating
to unlawful liquor sales activity.

(2) Municipal enforcement under this subsection shall
not require prior approval from, or coordination with, the
Bureau of Liquor Control Enforcement as a condition of
initiating enforcement activity.
(3) Training of municipal law enforcement officers under
this subsection, where applicable, shall be consistent with
standards established or recognized by the Bureau of Liquor
Control Enforcement. Municipalities may elect to participate
in training programs established or recognized by the Bureau
of Liquor Control Enforcement.
(4) Nothing in this section shall be construed to:
(i) transfer, diminish, expand or supersede the
primary enforcement authority of the Bureau of Liquor
Control Enforcement or the Pennsylvania State Police;
(ii) authorize routine administrative inspections of
licensed premises by municipal police officers;
(iii) authorize independent regulatory
investigations outside the scope of otherwise lawful
police activity;
(iv) authorize checkpoints without reasonable
suspicion of criminal activity;
(v) authorize other investigative activities not
otherwise authorized by law; or
(vi) authorize investigative or enforcement
authority by sheriffs or deputy sheriffs beyond authority
otherwise provided by law.
(5) Municipal law enforcement agencies shall provide
incident documentation relating to enforcement activity under
this section to the Bureau of Liquor Control Enforcement
within 48 hours.

(6) Nothing in this section shall be construed to create
a new cause of action or to expand civil liability for
municipalities or municipal law enforcement officers acting
within the scope of otherwise lawful duties.
(e) Definitions.--As used in this section, the following
words and phrases shall have the meanings given to them in this
subsection unless the context clearly indicates otherwise:
"Liquor." As defined in section 102 of the Liquor Code.
"Liquor Code." The act of April 12, 1951 (P.L.90, No.21),
known as the Liquor Code.
"Organized unlawful liquor sales operation." An unlawful
operation primarily engaged in repeated unlicensed liquor sales
activity for commercial gain and involving two or more of the
following factors:
(1) Collection of admission, entrance fee or membership
charges, whether collected in person, electronically,
digitally or through any other payment method.
(2) Employment of security personnel or controlled-entry
practices.
(3) Operation during hours prohibited under the Liquor
Code.
(4) Advertising or promotion of unlawful alcohol sales
activity.
(5) Use of a premises for recurring unlawful alcohol
sales activity.

Pa.C.S. § 7333.
