A RESOLUTION
Urging the Congress of the United States to establish terms of
18 years of regular active service for justices of the
Supreme Court of the United States.
WHEREAS, The Constitution of the United States, drafted and
signed in Philadelphia in 1787, divides the powers of government
among three coequal branches so that no branch may hold
authority beyond the reach of the others; and
WHEREAS, Article III of the Constitution of the United States
vests the judicial power of the United States in one Supreme
Court and in such inferior courts as Congress may establish, and
Congress has exercised its authority to organize the Supreme
Court throughout the history of the Nation, including by fixing
and adjusting the number of justices; and
WHEREAS, Justices of the Supreme Court hold their offices for
life, and because life expectancy has risen substantially since
the founding of the Nation, a single justice may now shape the
interpretation of the law for a generation or more; and
WHEREAS, In the years since Citizens United v. Federal
Election Commission (2010), the Supreme Court has issued a

series of decisions that reshaped the law governing campaign
finance, voting rights, redistricting, reproductive freedom,
firearms regulation and presidential power, including Shelby

Jackson Women's Health Organization (2022), New York State Rifle

WHEREAS, On April 29, 2026, in Louisiana v. Callais, the
Supreme Court, by a vote of 6 to 3, held that a congressional
district drawn to remedy a violation of section 2 of the Voting
Rights Act of 1965 (Public Law 89-110, 79 Stat. 437) was
unconstitutional and adopted a standard that substantially
narrows the ability of voters to challenge election maps that
dilute their political power; and
WHEREAS, Congress amended section 2 of the Voting Rights Act
of 1965 in 1982, with bipartisan support, to establish a
results-based standard after the Supreme Court's decision in
City of Mobile v. Bolden (1980), and the decision in Louisiana
v. Callais substantially restricts the operation of that
considered judgment of the people's elected representatives; and
WHEREAS, Within days of the decision in Louisiana v. Callais,
states moved to suspend congressional primary elections,
convened special legislative sessions and enacted new
congressional district maps, demonstrating how a single decision
of the Supreme Court can immediately disrupt the administration
of elections in the states; and
WHEREAS, Members of the Supreme Court have been the subject
of public controversy concerning ethics, financial disclosure
and recusal, and the confirmation of justices has become an
increasingly contentious contest because each vacancy may

WHEREAS, Public confidence in the Supreme Court has fallen to
historically low levels, and the legitimacy of the Court depends
on the trust of the public in the integrity and fairness of the
institution; and
WHEREAS, Terms of 18 years of regular active service,
staggered so that a vacancy arises every two years, would give
each President two appointments during a four-year term, would
reduce the incentive to time retirements for political
advantage, would bring new perspectives to the Supreme Court at
regular intervals and would lower the stakes of individual
confirmation proceedings; and
WHEREAS, Under the leading proposals, a justice who completes
18 years of regular active service would assume senior status
rather than leave judicial service entirely; and
WHEREAS, More than 50 constitutional scholars and retired
Federal and state judges have concluded that Congress may
establish term limits for justices by statute, legislation to do
so has been introduced in the 119th Congress, including S. 5076,
H.R. 1074 and H.R. 3544, and members of the Pennsylvania
congressional delegation have cosponsored such legislation; and
WHEREAS, No other major democracy grants life tenure to the
judges of its highest constitutional court, the highest court of
every state but one does not provide life tenure, and the
Constitution of Pennsylvania provides for ten-year terms,
subject to retention by the voters, and retirement at age 75 for
the justices of the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania; and
WHEREAS, Term limits for justices of the Supreme Court enjoy
broad public support among Democrats, Republicans and
independents alike; and

WHEREAS, The residents of Pennsylvania are directly affected
by the decisions of the Supreme Court of the United States, and
the Senate of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania has a proper
interest in the accountability of that institution; therefore be
it
RESOLVED, That the Senate of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania
urge the Congress of the United States to establish terms of 18
years of regular active service for justices of the Supreme
Court of the United States; and be it further
RESOLVED, That the Senate of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania
urge the Congress of the United States to enact legislation with
the terms staggered so that a vacancy arises every two years and
each President makes two appointments during a four-year term,
with senior status for justices who complete their terms; and be
it further
RESOLVED, That the Senate of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania
urge each member of Congress from Pennsylvania to support,
cosponsor and advance such legislation; and be it further
RESOLVED, That copies of this resolution be transmitted to
the President of the United States, the presiding officers of
each house of Congress, the chairs and ranking members of the
Committees on the Judiciary of the United States Senate and the
United States House of Representatives and each member of
Congress from Pennsylvania.
