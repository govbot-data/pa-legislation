creating a universal single-payer health care system;
providing for powers and duties of the Office of Health Care;
establishing the Pennsylvania Health Care Board; providing
for eligible participants; establishing the Health Care
Restricted Account; and providing for sources of revenue.

Section 3. Office of Health Care.
The Office of Health Care is established within the
department for the purpose of creating a universal single-payer
health care system in this Commonwealth. The office shall be
responsible for the collection and disbursement of money
required to provide covered services for each eligible
participant.
Section 4. Powers and duties of office.
The office shall have the following powers and duties:
(1) Make contracts and execute all instruments necessary
or convenient for implementing the purposes of this act.
(2) Enter into agreements or transactions with a
Federal, State or municipal agency or other public
institution or with a private individual, partnership, firm,
corporation, association or other entity.
(3) Appear on the office's own behalf before a board,
commission, department or other agency of the Federal
Government, the Commonwealth or a municipal government.
(4) Appoint officers and engage and employ employees,
including legal counsel, consultants, agents and advisors,
and specify their duties and fix their compensation.
(5) Establish advisory boards.

Section 7. Regional division.
(a) Establishment.--The regional division is established
within the office. The executive director shall appoint a
director of the regional division with the approval of the
board. The director of the regional division shall supervise and
control the regional division. The powers and duties of the
director of the regional division specified under this act or
any other law of this Commonwealth shall be exercised and
discharged subject to the control and supervision of the
executive director. The executive director may remove the
director of the regional division with the approval of the
board. The director of the regional division shall establish a
professional advisory committee to provide expert advice. The
professional advisory committee shall have at least 25%
representation from the general public.

number of regional offices located throughout this Commonwealth.

and location of the regional offices to the executive director
and the board after consultation with the directors of the
administrative, planning, quality assurance and information
technology divisions and with consideration of convenience and
equity. The board shall review the adequacy and appropriateness
of the number and location of the regional offices at least once
every three years.

Section 12. Eligible participants.
(a) Eligibility.--The following individuals may enroll as
eligible participants in the system:
(1) A resident.
(2) A nonresident who:
(i) works at least 20 hours per week in this
Commonwealth;
(ii) pays all applicable State personal income and
payroll taxes; and
(iii) pays an additional premium established by the
office to cover nonresidents.
(3) A nonresident patient requiring emergency treatment
for illness and injury. The office shall recoup expenses
incurred for a patient specified under this paragraph
wherever possible.
(b) Payments.--Payment for emergency care of residents
obtained out-of-State shall be at the prevailing local rates.
Payment for nonemergency care of residents obtained out-of-State
shall be in accordance with the rates and conditions established
by the executive director. The executive director may require
that a resident be transported back to this Commonwealth when

Section 13. Eligible health care practitioners or health care
facilities.
A health care practitioner or health care facility that is
eligible to participate in the system shall include an agency,
facility, corporation, individual or other entity directly
rendering a covered service to an eligible participant if the
health care practitioner or health care facility meets all of
the following criteria:
(1) The health care practitioner or health care facility
is licensed to practice or operate in this Commonwealth.
(2) The health care practitioner or health care facility
does not accept payment from other sources for services
provided for by the office.
(3) The health care practitioner or health care facility
furnishes a signed agreement to the office with the following
provisions:
(i) The health care practitioner or health care
facility shall provide health care services without
discrimination on the basis of factors, including age,
sex, race, national origin, sexual orientation, gender
identity, income status, preexisting condition or
citizenship status.

Section 15. Covered services.
(a) Payments.--The office shall pay for covered services
provided by a health care practitioner or health care facility
to an eligible participant as necessary to:
(1) provide high quality, appropriate and medically
necessary health care services;
(2) encourage reductions in health risks and increase
use of preventive and primary care services; and
(3) integrate physical, mental and behavioral health and
substance use services.

health care services determined to be medically necessary or
appropriate by the office, including any of the following:

blood products, dialysis, palliative care, dental care,
acupuncture, physical therapy and chiropractic and podiatric
services.

care through appropriate screening, counseling and health
education.
(3) The rehabilitation of individuals who are sick or
individuals with a disability, including physical,
psychological and other specialized therapies.
(4) Mental health services, including supportive
residences, occupational therapy and ongoing outpatient
services.
(5) Behavioral health services, including supportive
residences, occupational therapy and ongoing outpatient

Section 16. Wraparound coverage for Federal health programs.
Before obtaining a Federal program's waiver to receive
Federal money through the office, the office shall seek to
ensure that eligible participants who are also eligible for
Federal program coverage receive access to care and coverage
equal to that of all other residents. The office shall pay for
all of the following:
(1) Covered services specified under section 15 that are
not covered by the relevant Federal plans.
(2) Services during a federally mandated gap in an
eligible participant's coverage.
(3) Deductibles, copayments, coinsurance or other cost
sharing incurred by an eligible participant.

Section 20. Insurance reforms.
An insurer regulated by the Insurance Department may not
charge premiums to an eligible participant for covered services.
The Insurance Commissioner shall promulgate regulations and
adopt guidelines as necessary to implement this section.
Section 21. Implementation of office.
(a) Initial appointments.--No later than 60 days after the
effective date of this subsection, the Governor and Attorney
General shall make the initial appointments to the board.
(b) Hiring and budgeting process.--
(1) The board shall immediately begin the process of
hiring an executive director, reviewing enabling legislation
and educating itself regarding general purposes, economics
and authority of the office.
(2) The board shall develop a budget for the first year
of transition to the system and initiate the process of
obtaining Federal waivers and agreements concerning payments
from Medicare, Medicaid and other public programs.
(3) The board shall set a general time period for
establishing the office no earlier than one year and no later
than 18 months after the first meeting of the board.
(c) Transition phases.--
(1) In the first phase of transition to the system, the
executive director shall begin hiring staff, establishing the
administrative and information technology infrastructure for
the office and negotiating reimbursement rates for health
care services, pharmaceuticals and medical equipment. A
health care practitioner or health care facility shall
develop plans for transitioning to the system.
