AN ACT
Providing for reimbursement claims methodology and for safety
net reimbursement protection; providing for safety net
prescription drug distribution and access protections;
imposing duties on the Department of Human Services,
Insurance Department and Office of Attorney General; and
imposing penalties.
The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania
hereby enacts as follows:
Section 1. Short title.
This act shall be known and may be cited as the Safety Net
Nondiscrimination Act.

Section 3. Discriminatory pharmacy reimbursement prohibited.
(a) Payment.--Neither a pharmacy benefit manager nor a
covered insurer that reimburses a covered entity or a contract
pharmacy for drugs under a contract, or any agent acting on
behalf of a pharmacy benefit manager or covered insurer, shall
pay the covered entity or contract pharmacy less than:
(1) the national average drug acquisition cost as
described in 42 U.S.C. § 1396r-8(f) (relating to payment for
covered outpatient drugs) for 340B drugs; or
(2) the wholesale acquisition cost as specified in 42
U.S.C. § 1395w-3a(c)(6)(B) (relating to use of average sales
price payment methodology) for 340B drugs that do not have a
defined national average drug acquisition cost.
(b) Waiver.--Notwithstanding subsection (a), a covered
entity shall have the sole discretion to voluntarily agree to
waive the protections in this section, provided that a person
may not obtain a waiver through means of any direct or indirect
coercion. A covered entity may withdraw a waiver upon reasonable

Section 4. Reimbursement claims methodology.
The department, a covered insurer, a pharmacy benefit manager
or any other entity that administers or manages prescription
drug benefits on behalf of the department or a Medicaid managed
care organization, shall rely exclusively on a claims-level
methodology involving the use of a nationally recognized billing
modifier for identifying drugs purchased under section 340B of
the Public Health Service Act when paying for drugs on behalf of
Medicaid to ensure that claims for the drugs are removed from
the encounter data required under section 42 U.S.C. § 1396b(m)
(2)(A)(xiii) (relating to payment to States) and excluded from
Medicaid drug rebate requests.
Section 5. Safety net reimbursement protection.
(a) Prohibitions.--A Medicaid managed care organization, a
pharmacy benefit manager or other entity administering or
managing prescription drug benefits on behalf of a Medicaid
managed care organization may not:
(1) prevent a covered entity or contract pharmacy from
administering or dispensing 340B drugs by any legal means,
including mail delivery, or submitting a claim for
reimbursement for 340B drugs or decline to cover a claim for
reimbursement for administered or dispensed 340B drugs
because the drug was purchased under section 340B of the
Public Health Service Act;
(2) impose a limitation on the distance between the
covered entity and contract pharmacy or between the covered
entity or contract pharmacy and the patient; or
(3) except as necessary to comply with Federal or State
law or to prevent a duplicate discount prohibited under

least $500 from the department's Federal funding under
section 318 of the Public Health Service Act to perform
sexually transmitted disease testing and treatment.

Section 7. Penalties.
(a) Enforcement.--
(1) The Insurance Department shall investigate
complaints alleging violations and enforce sections 3 and 5
of this act and may examine or audit the books and records of
a pharmacy benefit manager, covered insurer or related party
to determine if the party is compliant with this act and
issue any subpoenas needed to carry out enforcement and
oversight under this act.
(2) The commission of any act that violates section 6
shall constitute an unlawful practice within the meaning of
the act of December 17, 1968 (P.L.1224, No.387), known as the
Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law. The
Office of Attorney General shall have jurisdiction,
consistent with the provisions of the Unfair Trade Practices
and Consumer Protection Law, to enforce the provisions of
section 6 and to promulgate regulations implementing the
provisions of section 6.

Section 9. Construction.
(a) Federal law.--Nothing in this act shall be construed or
applied to be less restrictive than any Federal law as to any
person or entity regulated by this act.

applied to be in conflict with any of the following:
(1) Applicable Federal law and related regulations,
including 42 U.S.C. Ch. 7 Subch. XI Pt. E. (relating to price
negotiation program to lower prices for certain high-priced
single source drugs).

act shall be interpreted to provide greater protection for

November 21, 2016 (P.L.1318, No.169), known as the Pharmacy
Benefit Reform Act, if both acts are applicable.
