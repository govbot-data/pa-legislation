establishing standards for frontier developers in addressing
critical safety incidents and catastrophic risks; imposing
duties on the Pennsylvania Emergency Management Agency and
the Attorney General; and imposing penalties.

CHAPTER 1
PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS
Section 101. Short title.
This act shall be known and may be cited as the Artificial
Intelligence Risk Prevention Act.
Section 102. Definitions.
The following words and phrases when used in this act shall
have the meanings given to them in this section unless the
context clearly indicates otherwise:
"Affiliate." A person controlling, controlled by or under
common control with a specified person, directly or indirectly,
through one or more intermediaries.
"Agency." The Pennsylvania Emergency Management Agency.
"Artificial intelligence" or "AI." As follows:
(1) An automated system that produces text, audio,
video, images, interactive responses or any other interaction
on a digital platform that represents or simulates a user.
(2) The term includes generative artificial
intelligence.
"Audit." An audit required under Chapter 7.
"Catastrophic risk." As follows:
(1) A foreseeable and material risk that a frontier
developer's development, storage, use or deployment of a
frontier model will materially contribute to the death of, or
serious injury to, more than 50 people or more than
$1,000,000,000 in damage to, or loss of, property arising
from a single incident involving a frontier model doing any
of the following:

(ii) Engaging in conduct without meaningful human

(A) is a cyberattack; or
(B) if the conduct had been committed by a

(iii) Evading the control of its frontier developer

shall not be considered as part of the damage to or loss of

"Foundation model." An artificial intelligence system that

(1) Trained on a broad data set.
(2) Designed for generality of output.
(3) Adaptable to a wide range of distinctive tasks.
"Frontier AI framework." Documented technical and
organizational protocols to manage, assess and mitigate a
catastrophic risk.
"Frontier developer." A person who trains, or initiates the
training of, a frontier model using computing power that meets
the technical specifications specified in the definition of
"frontier model."

"Frontier model." As follows:
(1) A foundation model that was trained using a
computing power greater than 1,026 integer or floating-point
operations.
(2) The quantity of computing power described in
paragraph (1) includes computing for the original training
run and for any subsequent fine-tuning, reinforcement
learning or other material modifications that the developer
applies to the foundation model.
"Generative artificial intelligence." The class of models
that emulates the structure and characteristics of input data in
order to generate derived synthetic content.
"Large frontier developer." A frontier developer that,
together with its affiliates, collectively had annual gross
revenues in excess of $500,000,000 in the preceding calendar
year.
"Model weight." A numerical parameter in a frontier model
that is adjusted through training and that helps determine how
inputs are transformed into outputs.
"Property." A tangible or intangible asset.
"Synthetic content." As follows:
(1) Information that has been significantly modified or
generated by algorithms, including by artificial
intelligence.
(2) The term includes images, videos, audio clips and
text.

CHAPTER 5
FRONTIER MODELS
Section 501. Frontier AI frameworks.
(a) Development.--Upon the effective date of this
subsection, each large frontier developer shall begin to develop
a frontier AI framework to govern how the large frontier
developer will:
(1) Incorporate national standards, international
standards and industry-consensus best practices into the
frontier AI framework.
(2) Establish thresholds to assess whether a frontier
model has capabilities that could pose a catastrophic risk,
which may include multiple-tiered thresholds.

Section 702. Standards.
A third party:
(1) Shall conduct an audit consistent with generally
accepted auditing standards and best practices.
(2) Must possess demonstrated competence to perform an
audit, including experience employing or contracting with
individuals who possess technical expertise in the safety of
frontier models.
Section 703. Financial interest.
A large frontier developer may not retain a third party to
perform an audit if the large frontier developer has a financial
interest in the third party or the third party has a financial
interest in the large frontier developer.
Section 704. Compensation.
A large frontier developer may compensate a third party for
services associated with performing an audit but may not
condition payment, or the amount of payment, for services on the
results of the third party's audit.

CHAPTER 9
CRITICAL SAFETY INCIDENTS
Section 901. Reporting mechanism for critical safety incidents.
(a) Establishment.--The agency, in consultation with the
Attorney General, shall establish a mechanism to be used by a
frontier developer or a member of the public to report a
critical safety incident.
(b) Information.--The mechanism described in subsection (a)
must include the ability to provide the following information:
(1) The date of the critical safety incident.
(2) The reasons that the incident qualifies as a
critical safety incident.
(3) A short and plain statement describing the critical
safety incident.
(4) Whether the critical safety incident was associated
with internal use of a frontier model.

(a) Duty to report.--Subject to subsection (c), within 72

poses an imminent risk of death or serious physical injury, the
frontier developer shall disclose the critical safety incident
within 24 hours to an appropriate authority, including a law
enforcement agency or public safety agency with jurisdiction,
based on the nature of the critical safety incident and as

(d) Amended reports.--A frontier developer that discovers
information about a critical safety incident after filing the
initial report required under this section may file an amended
report.
(e) Discretion to report.--A frontier developer is
encouraged, but not required, to report critical safety
incidents pertaining to foundation models that are not frontier
models.
Section 903. Review.
The agency and the Attorney General:
(1) Shall review critical safety incident reports
submitted by frontier developers.
(2) May review critical safety incident reports
submitted by members of the public.

A large frontier developer shall transmit to the agency each

Section 1103. Materially false or misleading statements.
(a) Catastrophic risk.--A frontier developer may not make a
materially false or misleading statement about catastrophic risk

not make a materially false or misleading statement about its
implementation of, or compliance with, its frontier AI
framework.
(c) Applicability.--This subsection does not apply to a
statement that was made in good faith and was reasonable under
the circumstances.

(a) Compliance generally.--Subject to subsection (b), a
person commits a violation of this act if the person:
(1) Develops, deploys or operates a frontier model in
this Commonwealth without having met all of the requirements
under this act.
(2) Submits materially false or misleading information
in any disclosure, report or assessment under this act.
(3) Fails to otherwise comply with this act.
(b) Large frontier developers.--A large frontier developer
commits a violation of this act if the large frontier developer:
(1) Fails to publish or transmit a compliant document
required to be published or transmitted under this act.
(2) Fails to comply with its own frontier AI framework.
(3) Fails to have a third party perform an independent
audit in accordance with Chapter 7.
(4) Fails to report a critical safety incident in
accordance with Chapter 9.
(5) Makes a materially false or misleading statement
under section 1103.

Section 1503. Construction.
The duties, obligations, rights and remedies under this act
are in addition to the duties, obligations, rights and remedies
that are under other applicable State law and shall not be
construed to:

other applicable State law; or

State law.
