Short title.This act shall be known and may be cited as the Jayanna Powell Hit-and-Run Direct Notification Pilot Program Act.Section 2. Legislative findings.The General Assembly finds and declares as follows:(1) Hit-and-run accidents, in which the driver flees the scene of an accident, result in the death of nearly 3,000 Americans each year according to the American Automobile Association. Approximately 60% of the victims of these accidents are pedestrians.(2) Fleeing the scene of an accident is prohibited under 75 Pa.C.S. §§ 3742 (relating to accidents involving death or personal injury) and 3744 (relating to duty to give 12345678910111213141516171819 information and render aid) due to the duty of motorists involved in an accident to render aid and provide information. It is in the best interest of the public for motorists involved in accidents that result in serious bodily injury or death to stay at the scene of an accident and to submit to authorities for questioning.(3) On November 18, 2016, a driver traveling at a high rate of speed struck eight-year-old Jayanna Powell of West Philadelphia as she walked home from school, resulting in her death. The driver fled the scene of the accident, leaving Jayanna's family aggrieved and without hope for justice.(4) The driver who struck Jayanna, aided by an accomplice, took the vehicle to a repair facility two counties away from Philadelphia so as to evade detection.(5) In order to more quickly identify and apprehend motorists who have fled the scene of an accident, the General Assembly finds it necessary to implement a system of direct notification to motor vehicle repair facilities of all vehicles involved in hit-and-run accidents.Section 3. The Jayanna Powell Hit-and-Run Direct Notification System Pilot Program.(a) Establishment.--The Jayanna Powell Hit-and-Run Direct Notification System Pilot Program is established in a city. A city shall develop and maintain, in collaboration with the law enforcement agency located in the city, a hit-and-run accident notification system, to be known as the Jayanna Powell Hit-and-Run Direct Notification System, that complies with this act. The city shall implement the system no later than six months after the effective date of this subsection.(b) Notification requirements.--After a hit-and-run accident resulting in serious bodily injury or death in a city, the investigating law enforcement agency of the city shall directly notify all motor vehicle repair facilities via the system. The system shall allow the notifications to be received on a cellular device or email.(c) Information.--The information provided on the system regarding the vehicle involved in a hit-and-run accident shall include, as available, the following:(1) Motor vehicle make, model, year and color.(2) Issuing jurisdiction of the license plate.(3) License plate number.(4) Unique identifying characteristics.20260SB1425PN1900 - 3 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 (5) Extent of damage.Section 5. Registration required.(a) Duty of motor vehicle repair facility.--A motor vehicle repair facility shall register with a city, in a manner prescribed by the governing body of the city, to ensure accurate contact information for providing information through the system.(b) Public advertisement.--The governing body of a city shall advertise the requirement to register, including advertising on a city's publicly accessible Internet website, and send direct notification to register to motor vehicle repair facilities via United States mail. Other reasonable efforts to provide direct notification of the requirement to register to motor vehicle repair facilities may be utilized in addition to the direct mail requirement.Section 6. Duty to report.If the owner or operator of a motor vehicle repair facility discovers that a vehicle in the motor vehicle repair facility's possession reasonably matches that of a vehicle described in a direct notification on the system, the owner or operator shall inform the law enforcement agency that initiated the direct notification within 72 hours.Section 7. Penalties.The owner or operator of a motor vehicle repair facility who fails to report a vehicle at the motor vehicle repair facility as required by this act commits a misdemeanor of the third degree.Section 8. The report shall include information regarding the costs associated with the system, the number of motor vehicle repair facilities registered, the number of alerts sent, the number of hit-and-run accidents that resulted in serious bodily injury or death, necessary system upgrades and any other information deemed relevant by the governing body of the city.Section 9. Expiration.The duties and obligations of a city and motor vehicle repair facilities under this act shall expire five years from the effective date of this section.Section 10.
