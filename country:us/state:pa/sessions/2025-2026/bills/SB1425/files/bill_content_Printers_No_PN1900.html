AN ACT
Establishing the Jayanna Powell Hit-and-Run Direct Notification
System Pilot Program in the city of the first class;
requiring registration of motor vehicle repair facilities;
and imposing penalties.
The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania
hereby enacts as follows:
Section 1. Short title.
This act shall be known and may be cited as the Jayanna
Powell Hit-and-Run Direct Notification Pilot Program Act.
Section 2. Legislative findings.
The General Assembly finds and declares as follows:
(1) Hit-and-run accidents, in which the driver flees the
scene of an accident, result in the death of nearly 3,000
Americans each year according to the American Automobile
Association. Approximately 60% of the victims of these
accidents are pedestrians.
(2) Fleeing the scene of an accident is prohibited under
75 Pa.C.S. §§ 3742 (relating to accidents involving death or
personal injury) and 3744 (relating to duty to give

(3) On November 18, 2016, a driver traveling at a high
rate of speed struck eight-year-old Jayanna Powell of West
Philadelphia as she walked home from school, resulting in her
death. The driver fled the scene of the accident, leaving
Jayanna's family aggrieved and without hope for justice.
(4) The driver who struck Jayanna, aided by an

counties away from Philadelphia so as to evade detection.
(5) In order to more quickly identify and apprehend
motorists who have fled the scene of an accident, the General
Assembly finds it necessary to implement a system of direct
notification to motor vehicle repair facilities of all
vehicles involved in hit-and-run accidents.

The following words and phrases when used in this act shall
have the meanings given to them in this section unless the
context clearly indicates otherwise:
"City." A city of the first class located in this
Commonwealth.
"Hit-and-run accident." A motor vehicle accident as provided
in 75 Pa.C.S. § 3742 (relating to accidents involving death or
personal injury) in which one or more motorists involved in the
accident fail to remain at the scene until law enforcement
authorities arrive or otherwise comply with the motorist duties

"System." The Jayanna Powell Hit-and-Run Direct Notification

Section 4. The Jayanna Powell Hit-and-Run Direct Notification
System Pilot Program.
(a) Establishment.--The Jayanna Powell Hit-and-Run Direct
Notification System Pilot Program is established in a city. A
city shall develop and maintain, in collaboration with the law
enforcement agency located in the city, a hit-and-run accident
notification system, to be known as the Jayanna Powell Hit-and-
Run Direct Notification System, that complies with this act. The
city shall implement the system no later than six months after
the effective date of this subsection.
(b) Notification requirements.--After a hit-and-run accident
resulting in serious bodily injury or death in a city, the
investigating law enforcement agency of the city shall directly
notify all motor vehicle repair facilities via the system. The
system shall allow the notifications to be received on a
cellular device or email.
(c) Information.--The information provided on the system
regarding the vehicle involved in a hit-and-run accident shall
include, as available, the following:
(1) Motor vehicle make, model, year and color.
(2) Issuing jurisdiction of the license plate.
(3) License plate number.
(4) Unique identifying characteristics.

Section 5. Registration required.
(a) Duty of motor vehicle repair facility.--A motor vehicle
repair facility shall register with a city, in a manner
prescribed by the governing body of the city, to ensure accurate
contact information for providing information through the
system.
(b) Public advertisement.--The governing body of a city
shall advertise the requirement to register, including
advertising on a city's publicly accessible Internet website,
and send direct notification to register to motor vehicle repair
facilities via United States mail. Other reasonable efforts to
provide direct notification of the requirement to register to
motor vehicle repair facilities may be utilized in addition to
the direct mail requirement.
Section 6. Duty to report.
If the owner or operator of a motor vehicle repair facility
discovers that a vehicle in the motor vehicle repair facility's
possession reasonably matches that of a vehicle described in a
direct notification on the system, the owner or operator shall
inform the law enforcement agency that initiated the direct
notification within 72 hours.
Section 7. Penalties.
The owner or operator of a motor vehicle repair facility who
fails to report a vehicle at the motor vehicle repair facility
as required by this act commits a misdemeanor of the third
degree.
