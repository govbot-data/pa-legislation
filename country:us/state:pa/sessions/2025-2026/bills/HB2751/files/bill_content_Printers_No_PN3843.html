§ 2501. Scope of subpart.
This subpart relates to professional and amateur mixed
martial arts.
§ 2502. Definitions.
The following words and phrases when used in this subpart
shall have the meanings given to them in this section unless the
context clearly indicates otherwise:
"Building." Includes an outdoor venue in which a competition
is held.
"Competition." A contest or exhibition.
"Contest." A fully sanctioned, competitive professional or
amateur bout involving mixed martial arts in which:
(1) contestants try to win the bout; and
(2) the outcome affects the official fight records of
the contestants.
"Contestant." An individual who engages in mixed martial
arts competition, either as a professional or as an amateur.
"Exhibition." A nonscoring, friendly sparring match
involving mixed martial arts:
(1) which is designed for entertainment purposes;
(2) in which an official winner is not declared; and
(3) in which the outcome does not affect the official
fight records of the contestants.
"Flagrant foul." An intentional foul, using a foul technique
under section 25B10(b) (relating to acts constituting fouls) or
25C12(b) (relating to acts constituting fouls) , that causes a

"Mixed martial arts." Competition involving the use, subject
to any applicable limitations specified under this subpart, of a
combination of techniques from different disciplines of the
martial arts, including grappling, kicking and striking.
"Official." Includes a judge, referee, timekeeper and
scorekeeper.
"Promoter."
(1) A person that produces, arranges or stages a
professional or amateur competition.
(2) For the purposes of paragraph (1), if the person is
a corporation, the term "person" includes an officer,
director, employee or stockholder of the corporation.
"Subcommission." The Subcommission on Mixed Martial Arts
established under section 25D01 (relating to Subcommission on
Mixed Martial Arts).
§ 2503. Applicability of other law.
Except as otherwise provided in this subpart, the provisions
of Subpart B (relating to boxing), as they relate to
professional boxers, and 58 Pa. Code Ch. 21 (relating to
professional boxing) apply to professional and amateur
contestants, promoters and other licensees.

§ 25A01. Supervision and authority.
Each professional and amateur competition shall be conducted
under the supervision and authority of the commission.
§ 25A02. Fees.
Except as otherwise provided in this chapter, the fees
specified under 58 Pa. Code Ch. 13 (relating to bonds and fees)
apply to mixed martial arts permits, contestants, promoters and
other licensees.
§ 25A03. Insurance.
Each professional and amateur contestant at a competition
shall be covered by insurance, which shall be established by
commission policy and paid for by the promoter of the
competition.

§ 25A05. Results of competition.
(a) End of competition.--A competition may end under the
following results:
(1) Through submission by:
(i) Physical tap out.
(ii) Verbal tap out.
(2) Technical knockout by the referee stopping the
contest.
(3) Decision by means of the scorecards, including
unanimous decision, split decision or majority decision.
(4) Draw.
(5) Technical decision.
(6) Technical draw.
(7) Disqualification.
(8) Forfeit.
(9) No contest.
(b) Referee and physician.--The referee and ringside
physician are:
(1) The sole arbiters of a competition.
(2) The only individuals authorized to enter the ring or
fenced area at any time during competition and stop a
competition.

§ 25A07. Promoters.
(a) License.--A promoter must be licensed by the commission.
(b) Bond.--A promoter shall execute and file a surety bond
with the commission of not less than $10,000.
(c) Notification.--A promoter of a competition shall notify
the commission in writing of the date, time and location of the
competition. The notice must be received by the commission at
least 10 days in advance of the competition.
(d) Approval.--A competition may not be held unless the
promoter of the competition receives written approval from the
commission to hold the competition.
(e) Lists.--A promoter shall submit a written list of the
following to the commission:
(1) All the scheduled match-ups of a competition,
including the full names, records and scheduled rounds for
all the contestants of the competition.
(2) All the officials who may be working at a
competition.
§ 25A08. Limits regarding competitions.
The commission reserves the right to limit the number of the
following:
(1) Competitions per month to ensure the proper
regulation of each competition.
(2) Mixed martial arts bouts scheduled for each
competition.
§ 25A09. Officials.
(a) Approval.--The commission must approve officials for
competitions.

§ 25B03. Duration of rounds.
Except with the written or oral permission of the commission
or the executive director:
(1) A nonchampionship professional contest may not
exceed three rounds of five minutes per round with a one-
minute rest period following each round.
(2) A championship professional contest may not exceed
five rounds of five minutes per round with a one-minute rest
period following each round.

contestants in competitions are as follows:
(1) Flyweight, which is 125 pounds or less.
(2) Bantamweight, which is more than 125 pounds but 135
pounds or less.
(3) Featherweight, which is more than 135 pounds but 145
pounds or less.
(4) Lightweight, which is more than 145 pounds but 155
pounds or less.
(5) Welterweight, which is more than 155 pounds but 170
pounds or less.
(6) Middleweight, which is more than 170 pounds but 185
pounds or less.
(7) Light heavyweight, which is more than 185 pounds but
205 pounds or less.
(8) Heavyweight, which is more than 205 pounds but 265
pounds or less.
(9) Super heavyweight, which is more than 265 pounds.

§ 25B05. Proper attire.
(a) Trunks.--Each professional contestant shall wear mixed
martial arts shorts, biker shorts, boxing shorts or kickboxing
shorts during competition as approved by the commission.
(b) Shirt or gi.--
(1) A male professional contestant may not wear a shirt
or gi during competition.
(2) A female professional contestant shall wear a body
shirt and blouse during competition.
(c) Shoes.--A professional contestant may not wear shoes or
padding on the feet during competition.
§ 25B06. Physical appearance.
(a) Cleanliness.--Each professional contestant shall present
a clean appearance.
(b) Grease or foreign substance.--
(1) The excessive use of grease or any other foreign
substance, including grooming creams, lotions or sprays, may
not be used on the hair or any portion of the body of a
professional contestant.
(2) The referee or the commission shall cause any
excessive grease or foreign substance to be removed from the
hair or body of a professional contestant.
(3) A light coating of petroleum jelly is permitted on
the face only of a professional contestant. The coating:
(i) Must be applied at cage side or ring side in the
presence of the referee or the commission.
(ii) Is at the discretion of the referee.

§ 25B09. Scoring.
(a) Evaluation.--Judges in professional competitions shall
evaluate professional mixed martial arts techniques in the
following order:
(1) Effective striking.
(2) Effective grappling.
(3) Fighting area control.
(4) Effective aggressiveness/defense.
(b) Definitions.--As used in this section, the following
words and phrases shall have the meanings given to them in this
subsection unless the context clearly indicates otherwise:
"Effective aggressiveness/defense." Moving forward and
landing a legal strike, while avoiding being struck, taken down
or reversed, or both, while performing the maneuvers described
in this subsection.
"Effective grappling." The successful execution of a legal
takedown or reversal, or both, including the following
maneuvers:
(1) Takedowns from standing position to mount position.
(2) Passing the guard to mount position.
(3) Bottom position fighters using an active,
threatening guard.
"Effective striking." The total number of legal heavy
strikes landed.
"Fighting area control." Dictating the pace, location and
position using the following maneuvers:
(1) Countering an opponent's attempt at takedown to
remain standing and legally striking effectively.

accidentally fouled the professional contestant by hitting in

the ring or fenced area or otherwise indicates an unwillingness
to continue after a maximum of a five-minute rest period,
because of a claim of being hit in the groin, the competition
shall be declared to be a technical knockout in favor of the
contestant who is willing to continue.

contestant unable to continue the competition after the
completed second round of a nonchampionship competition, or
after the completed third round of a championship
competition, the outcome shall be determined by scoring the
completed rounds and the round during which the referee stops
the competition.
(2) If an injury inflicted by an accidental foul later
becomes aggravated by fair blows and the referee orders the
competition stopped because of the injury, the outcome shall
be determined by scoring the completed rounds and the round
during which the referee stops the competition.

§ 25C01. Applicability of Chapter 25B.
Except as otherwise provided in this chapter, the provisions
of Chapter 25B (relating to professional mixed martial arts)
apply to amateur mixed martial arts under this chapter.

of age.
(b) Debut amateur contestants.--
(1) A debut amateur contestant shall complete a
commission-approved form detailing the amateur contestant's
experience and training in mixed martial arts competitions.

(c) Professional boxer.--A professional boxer may compete as
an amateur in a mixed martial arts event only with the approval

§ 25C03. Licenses.
(a) License required.--An amateur contestant shall be
licensed by the commission.
(b) Review of information.--As part of its determination
whether to issue or renew a license for an amateur contestant,
the commission may review the applicant's experience,
qualifications, medical records, win-loss record and training
record.
(c) Fee.--A nonrefundable annual license fee of $10 must
accompany each application for a license or renewal of a license
as an amateur contestant.
§ 25C04. Prebout meeting.
Each amateur contestant shall attend a prebout meeting with a
designee of the commission to review all rules and regulations
of the commission pertaining to amateur mixed martial arts.
§ 25C05. Conduct of contests.
(a) Separate events.--Contests of amateur and professional
mixed martial arts shall be conducted as separate events.

(d) Timekeepers.--Each amateur competition must have at
least two timekeepers.
(e) Ruling of judge.--The ruling of a judge in an amateur
competition may not be overturned.

§ 25C07. Proper attire.
(a) Trunks.--Each amateur contestant shall wear mixed
martial arts shorts, biker shorts, boxing shorts or kickboxing
shorts during competition as approved by the commission.
(b) Shirt or gi.--
(1) A male amateur contestant may not wear a shirt or gi
during competition, other than a close-fitting rash guard. A
rash guard must be approved by the commission.
(2) A female amateur contestant shall wear a body shirt
and blouse during competition.
(c) Shoes.--An amateur contestant may not wear shoes or
padding on the feet during competition.
§ 25C08. Jewelry prohibited.
During competition, an amateur contestant may not wear
jewelry, including an earring, piercing or necklace.

replaced.

§ 25C10. Facial hair.
(a) Determination.--The commission shall determine whether
the head or facial hair of an amateur contestant:
(1) presents a hazard to the safety of the amateur
contestant or the opponent of the amateur contestant; or
(2) will interfere with the supervision and conduct of
the competition.
(b) Participation.--If the commission determines that either
of the circumstances under subsection (a) exists, the amateur
contestant may not participate in the competition unless the
circumstance is resolved to the satisfaction of the commission.
(c) Alternatives.--Without limiting the standard in this
section, head hair must be trimmed or tied back in a manner that
will not interfere with the vision of either amateur contestant
or cover any part of an amateur contestant's face.
§ 25C11. Legal techniques.
(a) While standing.--The following acts constitute legal
techniques while standing in a contest or exhibition of amateur
mixed martial arts:
(1) Closed hand strikes to head and body.
(2) Kicking to body and legs.
(3) Knees to body and legs.
(4) Throws.
(5) Takedowns.
(6) Sweeps.
(7) Standing submissions.
(8) Armbars, but only for takedowns.
(9) Shouldering.

§ 25C12. Acts constituting fouls.
(a) Disqualification.--Disqualification from an amateur
competition occurs after any combination of two fouls or after a
flagrant foul as determined by the referee.
(b) Acts.--The following acts constitute fouls in an amateur
competition:
(1) An act prohibited by section 25B10 (relating to acts
constituting fouls).
(2) Any of the following illegal techniques while
standing:
(i) Elbows of any kind.
(ii) Knees to head or groin.
(iii) Kicks to knees, head or groin.
(iv) Stomps on feet.
(v) Slamming. Throws should be completed in a
controlled manner.
(vi) Throws against a joint.
(vii) Neck cranks.
(viii) Pinching, biting or gouging.
(3) Any of the following illegal techniques while on the
ground:
(i) Striking to the head.
(ii) Neck cranks.
(iii) Heel hooks.
(iv) Finger locks.

§ 25C13. Age brackets.
In addition to the weight requirements under section 25C14
(relating to weight), an amateur contest may only be sanctioned
if both the amateur contestants in the amateur contest are in
one of the following age brackets:
(1) 12 years of age or older but less than 14 years of
age.
(2) 14 years of age or older but less than 16 years of
age.
(3) 16 years of age or older but less than 18 years of
age.
(4) 18 years of age or older but less than 21 years of
age.
(5) 21 years of age or older.
§ 25C14. Weight.
(a) Minimum weight.--An amateur contestant must weigh at
least 95 pounds.
(b) Weight classes.--In addition to meeting the age
requirements under section 25C13 (relating to age brackets), an
amateur contest may only be sanctioned if both the amateur
contestants in the amateur contest are in one of the following
weight classes:
(1) 95 pounds or more but less than 101 pounds.

§ 25C15. Medical information and testing.
(a) Medical passbook.--Each amateur contestant shall have an
up-to-date personal medical passbook in the form of a personal
logbook, mobile app or digital card, which can be used to track
the amateur contestant's chronic conditions, medications and
health records, including vital medical history, allergies,
surgeries and test results.
(b) Database.--
(1) The commission, in consultation with the
subcommission, shall establish, maintain and update a
database consisting of information from the medical passbooks
of the amateur contestants, as described in subsection (a).
(2) Information in the database shall not be public
information and shall only be accessible to:
(i) The commission.

accordance with the rules established by the commission and
subject to the approval of the secretary.

as a member by the Governor upon clear and convincing evidence
of misfeasance or malfeasance in office or neglect of duty. The
Governor shall provide the removed member with a detailed
written statement of the reasons for removal.
