andWHEREAS, This historic document was created during the dawn of American independence and stood as one of the most radically democratic and progressive state constitutions of the Revolutionary era; andWHEREAS, By 1776, many Pennsylvanians supported overthrow of elite control of the Pennsylvania General Assembly for a more democratic body; andWHEREAS, On May 15, 1776, the Continental Congress, which served as the provisional government and legislative body that coordinated the 13 colonies' resistance to British rule and led the United States through the American Revolutionary War, issued a resolution to the colonies to call constitutional conventions and write new constitutions; and123456789101112131415161718 WHEREAS, On June 18, 1776, in Carpenters' Hall, Philadelphia, there was a call for a constitutional convention "to form a Government for the Province of Pennsylvania, under the authority of the people"; andWHEREAS, On July 15, 1776, a Constitutional Convention was formed in Philadelphia with Benjamin Franklin serving as president, George Ross serving as vice president and John Morris serving as secretary; andWHEREAS, As one Pennsylvania delegate explained, the Convention was determined not to pay the least regard to former Constitutions and was resolved to reject everything...to clear away every part of the old rubbish...and begin upon a clean foundation; andWHEREAS, The Constitution of Pennsylvania of 1776 established a profound precedent by expanding voting rights to all tax-paying freemen, eliminating restrictive property ownership requirements and creating a strong Declaration of Rights that predated the United States Bill of Rights; andWHEREAS, The foundational principles of freedom, equality and self-governance laid down in 1776 served as a vital template for other states; andWHEREAS, The 1776 Constitution of Pennsylvania featured a unicameral legislature, a supreme executive council instead of a Governor and expanded voting rights; andWHEREAS, It also established a unique Council of Censors meant to monitor government adherence to the Constitution; andWHEREAS, By 1790, this system proved too chaotic and unbalanced, leading to calls for a more structured government and the first of five revisions of the Constitution of Pennsylvania; andWHEREAS, Following the Civil War, industrialization and rapid corporate growth led to widespread corruption within State government, which the 1874 Constitution of Pennsylvania aimed to curb; andWHEREAS, The 1874 Constitution of Pennsylvania lengthened the Governor's term, established strict regulations against legislative corruption and corporate favoritism and created the office of Lieutenant Governor; andWHEREAS, As society modernized, the 1874 Constitution of Pennsylvania became heavily cluttered and outdated; andWHEREAS, The 1968 Constitution of Pennsylvania streamlined government and vastly improved structural clarity;
