WARD, STREET, TARTAGLIONE, HUGHES, VOGEL, PHILLIPS-HILL, COSTA, MARTIN, CULVER AND HAYWOOD, OCTOBER 8, 2026 REFERRED TO RULES AND EXECUTIVE NOMINATIONS, OCTOBER 8, 2026 A RESOLUTIONDesignating the week of October 25 through 31, 2026, as "Epidermolysis Bullosa Awareness Week" in Pennsylvania.WHEREAS, Epidermolysis Bullosa (EB) is a rare, genetic, connective tissue disorder that is characterized by fragile skin and recurrent blister formation, inside and outside the body and, today, there is no cure; andWHEREAS, Severe forms of EB cause patients to live with constant pain and scarring, and the worst forms of EB lead to eventual disfigurement, disability and often early death; andWHEREAS, Phenotypes and outcomes of EB run the full scope, from mild to severe, with the more severe cases causing fatalities in the first year of life; andWHEREAS, In many cases, children who suffer from the more severe forms of EB do not live into their third decade of life; andWHEREAS, There are five types of EB: EB simplex, Junctional EB, Dystrophic EB, Kindler EB and EB Acquisita; and1234567891011121314151617 WHEREAS, EB simplex is the most common form of EB, while the other forms are relatively less common and affect the patient more severely; andWHEREAS, The presence and type of EB is differentiated by the gene that is affected and the particular protein that is produced by that gene, therefore, EB can be the result of a mutation in genes that produce 1 of 18 different structural proteins; andWHEREAS, It has been estimated that 1 out of every 20,000 births, approximately 200 children a year, is affected with some type of EB, and the disorder occurs in every group throughout the world, affecting both sexes equally; andWHEREAS, With skin as fragile as a butterfly wing, EB patients are dubbed "Butterfly Children"; andWHEREAS, The awareness week for EB is commonly referred to as "Butterfly Children Awareness Week"; andWHEREAS, On the outside, physical wounds prevent them from normal daily activities enjoyed by other children as the slightest friction produces agonizing wounds that can cover up to 75% of their bodies; andWHEREAS, Unfortunately, there is a lack of long-term research surrounding EB, and most physicians, nationally and internationally, are unaware of the disorder and its complexities, which results in less than optimal treatment options for even the most common secondary complications;
