Short title.This act shall be known and may be cited as the Single-Family Residence Access Act.Section 2. Legislative findings and declarations.(a) Findings.--The General Assembly finds and declares as follows:(1) Covered entities buying up properties, often at costs affordable for these entities but costs that are too high for individuals, does not make housing affordable.(2) This increases the costs of these homes on the market or rent, inhibiting the ability for hardworking 1234567891011121314151617 residents of this Commonwealth to compete with these covered entities or to purchase or rent a home from these corporations as the corporations drive up the costs of the properties.(3) Efforts by the Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency to provide affordable loans to assist individuals buying homes date back to the 1980s, and these loans continue.(4) With the cost of real estate increasing, the Commonwealth needs to take additional action to ensure that homes are affordable for residents of this Commonwealth.(5) In a study from 2020, the agency found that most households own their home, however, the number of households renting continues to increase.(6) By putting restrictions on covered entities buying homes, the General Assembly seeks to ensure that residents of this Commonwealth have opportunities to purchase or rent a home within their financial means.(7) This act is intended to provide opportunities for residents of this Commonwealth to access affordable housing and shall be implemented to address concerns about covered entities buying up homes and exponentially increasing the cost to purchase or rent these properties.(8) In the interest of ensuring the availability of affordable housing for residents of this Commonwealth, the General Assembly finds that it is necessary to place restrictions on how easily covered entities can buy up homes before hardworking individuals. It is crucial that affordable housing is available to residents of this Commonwealth first and foremost.(b) Declaration.--The General Assembly declares that 20260HB2781PN3890 - 2 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 paramount among the objectives of this act, to which all other objectives and purposes are necessary, is to keep housing affordable for the residents of this Commonwealth. Other principal objectives are to:(1) Ensure that residents of this Commonwealth have the opportunity to purchase homes first and foremost.(2) Provide transparency by requiring each covered entity to disclose the entity's status as a covered entity under this act.(c) Intent.--The intent of this act is to:(1) Set requirements on a covered entity seeking to purchase a single-family residence.(2) Provide transparency to identify if a buyer is a covered entity under this act.Section 3. A residential structure designed for long-term occupation by one family or household, including detached single-family residences, condominiums, duplexes and townhouses.Section 4. Limitations on the purchase of single-family residences.A covered entity shall not purchase, acquire or offer to purchase or acquire a fee simple title to a single-family residence unless the single-family residence:(1) Will be used as the principal residence of a person with an ownership interest in the covered entity.(2) Was constructed or will be purchased, acquired or operated with money from the Federal, State or local government.(3) Is occupied by a tenant as defined in section 102 of the act of April 6, 1951 (P.L.69, No.20), known as The Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951.(4) Was built for occupancy by a tenant as defined in section 102 of The Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951.(5) Is not publicly listed for sale at the time the covered entity makes an offer on the single-family residence.(6) Is acquired through a judicial or nonjudicial foreclose sale, forfeiture, a deed in lieu of foreclosure or the enforcement of a judgment, lien, debt or property interest.(7) Has been publicly listed for sale for at least 90 consecutive days immediately preceding the covered entity first offering or accepting an offer to purchase or acquire any interest in the single-family residence.20260HB2781PN3890 - 5 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 Section 5. Disclosure of status of covered entity.(a) Creation of disclosure form.--The commission shall develop and publish a form to disclose the status of a covered entity.(b) Disclosure to agents.--Upon making or prior to accepting an offer to purchase or acquire a single-family residence under section 3, a covered entity shall complete a form to disclose to the seller or the seller's agent and the Office of the Attorney General that the buyer of the single-family residence is a covered entity before an agreement of transfer is signed.(c) Publication.--Completed forms submitted to the Office of the Attorney General shall be posted on the Office of the Attorney General's publicly accessible Internet website.Section 6. Penalties.The Office of the Attorney General may prosecute a civil action against a covered entity that fails to comply with the requirements to comply with the 90-day waiting period or complete the disclosure form published by the commission.Section 7.
