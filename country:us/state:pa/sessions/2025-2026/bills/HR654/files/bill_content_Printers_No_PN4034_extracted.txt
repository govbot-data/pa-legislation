andWHEREAS, The National Baseball Hall of Fame awards the Buck O'Neil Lifetime Achievement Award no more than once every three years to an individual whose extraordinary qualities and contributions to the sport are in keeping with those of Buck O'Neil; andWHEREAS, Bill White was born January 28, 1934, in Lakewood, Florida, was raised in Warren, Ohio, and is a longtime resident of Bucks County, Pennsylvania; andWHEREAS, Bill White's trailblazing baseball career started in 1953 in the Carolina League, where he significantly contributed 1234567891011121314151617 to integration efforts within the minor league over the next three years; andWHEREAS, He went on to play in the major league in 1956 with the New York Giants, followed by the St. Louis Cardinals in 1959 and, for three seasons starting in 1966, the Philadelphia Phillies; andWHEREAS, Bill White helped lead the St. Louis Cardinals to the World Series title in 1964; andWHEREAS, Bill White was selected for Major League Baseball's (MLB) All-Star Game eight times and won seven Gold Glove awards from 1960 through 1966 for his position as first baseman in the National League; andWHEREAS, His successful career in sports broadcasting and reporting started during his time as a player and included a stint at WFIL-TV, now WPVI-TV, in Philadelphia during his time with the Phillies; andWHEREAS, Postretirement, Bill White served as WFIL-TV's sports director for a year before being hired as a broadcaster for the New York Yankees, where he worked for nearly two decades; andWHEREAS, His radio position with the New York Yankees made Bill White the first Black play-by-play broadcaster for any MLB team; andWHEREAS, From 1989 to 1994, Bill White served not only as the first Black National League President, but also as the first former player to hold that office in 70 years; andWHEREAS, During his time as National League President, Bill White was instrumental in the National League expansion that added the Colorado Rockies and the Miami Marlins;
