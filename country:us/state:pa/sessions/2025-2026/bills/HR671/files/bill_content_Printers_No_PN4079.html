A RESOLUTION
Directing the Legislative Budget and Finance Committee to
conduct a study on the intergenerational impact the Carlisle
Indian Industrial School had on indigenous children, their
descendants and their tribal nations and to issue a report.
WHEREAS, The Carlisle Indian Industrial School, founded in
1879 at Carlisle Barracks in Cumberland County, was the first
Federal off-reservation boarding school for indigenous children
and operated until its closure on September 1, 1918; and
WHEREAS, The school's founder and superintendent, Richard
Henry Pratt, summarized the ethos of the policy as "kill the
Indian in him, and save the man"; and
WHEREAS, The purpose of the school was to assimilate
indigenous children by stripping them of their languages,
religions and cultures; and
WHEREAS, Students at Carlisle Indian Industrial School had
their hair cut and were forbidden to speak their native
languages; and
WHEREAS, Thousands of American Indian and Alaska Native
children from more than 140 tribal nations were enrolled at the

WHEREAS, Carlisle Indian Industrial School served as the
model for the Federal Indian boarding school system, which grew
to more than 400 Federal schools alongside hundreds of similar
institutions operated by religious organizations; and
WHEREAS, Approximately 180 children died while attending the
school, many of whom were buried at what is now the Carlisle
Barracks Main Post Cemetery, which the United States Army
relocated from its original site in 1927; and
WHEREAS, Since 2017, tribal nations and families have worked
with the United States Army to repatriate the remains of the
children buried at the now Carlisle Barracks Main Post Cemetery,
and those efforts are ongoing; and
WHEREAS, Under the Carlisle Indian Industrial School's outing
system, students were sent to live and work in the homes, on the
farms and in the businesses of non-Native families and
employers, including in this Commonwealth, providing a source of
low-wage labor; and
WHEREAS, Members of religious congregations in this
Commonwealth served as outing placements during the school's
years of operation; and
WHEREAS, The Federal Indian boarding school system, which
continued through the 1960s using practices first employed at
Carlisle Indian Industrial School, inflicted individual,
collective and multigenerational trauma on indigenous nations
and communities; and
WHEREAS, The United States Department of the Interior's
Federal Indian Boarding School Initiative, in its July 2024
investigative report, identified 417 Federal Indian boarding
schools across 37 states or then-territories and confirmed that

WHEREAS, On October 25, 2024, the President of the United
States issued a formal apology for use of the Federal Indian
boarding school system, and on December 9, 2024, established the
Carlisle Federal Indian Boarding School National Monument; and
WHEREAS, Institutions of this Commonwealth, including
Millersville University and the Episcopal Dioceses of Central
Pennsylvania and Bethlehem, have begun examining their own
connections to the school; and
WHEREAS, Federal investigations have centered on the conduct
of the Federal Government, and a full accounting of the role
played by the Commonwealth and by this Commonwealth's religious,
academic, philanthropic and commercial institutions requires
examination by the Commonwealth itself; therefore be it
RESOLVED, That the House of Representatives direct the
Legislative Budget and Finance Committee to conduct a study on
the intergenerational impact the Carlisle Indian Industrial
School had on indigenous children, their descendants and tribal
nations and to issue a report; and be it further
RESOLVED, That the study include, at a minimum, an
examination of the following:
(1) The removal of indigenous children from their
families and nations to the school, the conditions they
experienced there and the deaths and burials of children who
did not return home.
(2) The intergenerational impact of the school on former
students, their descendants and their tribal nations,
including impacts on language, culture, family structure,
health and economic well-being.
(3) The role of the Commonwealth, including its
