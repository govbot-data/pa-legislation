intergenerational impact the Carlisle Indian Industrial School had on indigenous children, their descendants and their tribal nations and to issue a report.WHEREAS, The Carlisle Indian Industrial School, founded in 1879 at Carlisle Barracks in Cumberland County, was the first Federal off-reservation boarding school for indigenous children and operated until its closure on September 1, 1918; andWHEREAS, The school's founder and superintendent, Richard Henry Pratt, summarized the ethos of the policy as "kill the Indian in him, and save the man"; andWHEREAS, The purpose of the school was to assimilate indigenous children by stripping them of their languages, religions and cultures; andWHEREAS, Students at Carlisle Indian Industrial School had their hair cut and were forbidden to speak their native languages; andWHEREAS, Thousands of American Indian and Alaska Native children from more than 140 tribal nations were enrolled at the 12345678910111213141516171819 school; andWHEREAS, Carlisle Indian Industrial School served as the model for the Federal Indian boarding school system, which grew to more than 400 Federal schools alongside hundreds of similar institutions operated by religious organizations; andWHEREAS, Approximately 180 children died while attending the school, many of whom were buried at what is now the Carlisle Barracks Main Post Cemetery, which the United States Army relocated from its original site in 1927; andWHEREAS, Since 2017, tribal nations and families have worked with the United States Army to repatriate the remains of the children buried at the now Carlisle Barracks Main Post Cemetery, and those efforts are ongoing; andWHEREAS, Under the Carlisle Indian Industrial School's outing system, students were sent to live and work in the homes, on the farms and in the businesses of non-Native families and employers, including in this Commonwealth, providing a source of low-wage labor; andWHEREAS, Members of religious congregations in this Commonwealth served as outing placements during the school's years of operation; andWHEREAS, The Federal Indian boarding school system, which continued through the 1960s using practices first employed at Carlisle Indian Industrial School, inflicted individual, collective and multigenerational trauma on indigenous nations and communities; andWHEREAS, The United States Department of the Interior's Federal Indian Boarding School Initiative, in its July 2024 investigative report, identified 417 Federal Indian boarding schools across 37 states or then-territories and confirmed that
