AN ACT
Amending the act of September 27, 1961 (P.L.1700, No.699),
entitled "An act relating to the regulation of the practice
of pharmacy, including the sales, use and distribution of
drugs and devices at retail; and amending, revising,
consolidating and repealing certain laws relating thereto,"
further providing for definitions; providing for HIV
infection prevention medication; and further providing for
collaborative drug therapy management and for construction.
The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania
hereby enacts as follows:
Section 1. Section 2(11) and (14) of the act of September
27, 1961 (P.L.1700, No.699), known as the Pharmacy Act, are
amended and the section is amended by adding clauses to read:

(4) A pharmacist may initiate, dispense or administer
preexposure prophylaxis HIV medication only if all of the
following apply:
(i) The patient is HIV negative, as documented by a negative
test result obtained within the previous seven days using an HIV
antigen/antibody test, an HIV antibody-only test or a rapid,
point-of-care fingerstick blood test approved by the United
States Food and Drug Administration. If the patient does not
provide evidence of a negative test result in accordance with
this subclause, the pharmacist shall order an HIV-related test.
If the test result is not transmitted directly to the
pharmacist, the pharmacist shall verify the test result to the
pharmacist's satisfaction. If the patient tests positive for HIV
infection, the pharmacist shall refer the patient to a medical
practitioner or clinic that provides primary care or HIV care
and provide the patient with a list of medical practitioners and
clinics in the region that provide primary care or HIV care.
(ii) The patient does not report any sign or symptom of
acute HIV infection on a self-reported checklist of signs and
symptoms of acute HIV infection.
(iii) The patient does not report taking medication that is
contraindicated for use with the preexposure prophylaxis HIV
medication.

regarding the ongoing use of the preexposure prophylaxis HIV
medication. The counseling may include education regarding side
effects, safety during pregnancy and breastfeeding, adherence to
recommended dosing and the importance of timely testing and
treatment, as applicable, for HIV, renal function, hepatitis B,

Section 9.3. Collaborative Drug Therapy Management.--(a)
[A] Except as provided under section 3.5, a pharmacist shall
enter into a written collaborative agreement with a licensed
physician authorizing the management of drug therapy for a
disease or for a condition or symptom of a disease before
practicing the management of drug therapy in a setting other
than an institutional setting.
* * *
Section 9.4. Construction.--[Nothing] Except as provided
under section 3.5, nothing in this act shall be construed to
provide prescriptive authority to a pharmacist.
Section 4. This act shall take effect immediately.
