HIV Infection Prevention Medication.--(a) A pharmacist may initiate, dispense or administer HIV infection prevention medication without a prescription in accordance with the Statewide drug therapy protocol and practice guidelines established under subsection (b) and the applicable requirements specified under subsection (c).(b) The board shall promulgate regulati ons establishing a 20260SB1462PN1949 - 3 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 Statewide drug therapy protocol and practice guidelines under which a pharmacist may initiate, dispense or administer HIV infection prevention medication. The protocol and guidelines shall include all of the following:(1) Satisfactory completion of a curriculum or training program approved by the board and offered by a provider accredited by the Accreditation Council for Pharmacy Education. The curriculum or training program must relate to the initiation, dispensing or administration of HIV infection prevention medication and related laboratory testing, assessment, referral and consultation.(2) Maintenance of professional liability insurance coverage in the minimum amount of one million dollars ($1,000,000) per occurrence or claims made. Failure to maintain insurance coverage as required under this paragraph shall be actionable under section 5. The board shall accept any of the following as satisfactory evidence of insurance coverage:(i) Personally purchased professional liability insurance.(ii) Professional liability insurance coverage provided by the pharmacist's employer.(iii) Similar insurance coverage acceptable to the board.(3) The pharmacist shall determine the minimum quantity of preexposure prophylaxis HIV medication necessary for an initial supply. The pharmacist may not dispense an initial supply of preexposure prophylaxis HIV medication to the same patient more than once every two years unless a medical practitioner authorized by law to prescribe preexposure prophylaxis HIV medication directs otherwise. If the patient does not provide evidence of a negative test result in accordance with this subclause, the pharmacist shall order an HIV-related test. If the test result is not transmitted directly to the pharmacist, the pharmacist shall verify the test result to the pharmacist's satisfaction. If the patient tests positive for HIV infection, the pharmacist shall refer the patient to a medical practitioner or clinic that provides primary care or HIV care and provide the patient with a list of medical practitioners and clinics in the region that provide primary care or HIV care.(ii) The patient does not report any sign or symptom of acute HIV infection on a self-reported checklist of signs and symptoms of acute HIV infection.(iii) The patient does not report taking medication that is contraindicated for use with the preexposure prophylaxis HIV medication.(iv) The pharmacist provides counseling to the patient regarding the ongoing use of the preexposure prophylaxis HIV medication.
