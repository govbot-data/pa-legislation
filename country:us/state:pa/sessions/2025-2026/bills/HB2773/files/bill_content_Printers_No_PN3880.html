hereby enacts as follows:

Statutes is amended by adding a chapter to read:

"Administrator." The term shall mean the same as defined in
section 1002 of the act of May 17, 1921 (P.L.789, No.285), known
as The Insurance Department Act of 1921.
"Health care provider." An individual who is authorized to
practice some component of the healing arts by a license,
permit, certificate or registration issued by a Commonwealth
licensing agency or board.
"Health insurance policy." An individual or group insurance
policy, subscriber contract, certificate or plan issued by an
insurer that provides medical or health care coverage, including
emergency services. The term does not include:
(1) An accident only policy.
(2) A credit only policy.
(3) A long-term care or disability income policy.
(4) A specified disease policy.
(5) A Medicare supplement policy.
(6) A TRICARE policy, including a Civilian Health and
Medical Program of the Uniformed Services (CHAMPUS)
supplement policy.
(7) A fixed indemnity policy.
(8) A hospital indemnity policy.
(9) A dental only policy.
(10) A vision only policy.
(11) A workers' compensation policy.
(12) An automobile medical payment policy.
(13) A homeowners' insurance policy.
(14) A short-term limited duration policy.
(15) Any other policy providing for limited benefits.

(a) Unilateral modification of contract.--An insurer or
administrator acting on behalf of an insurer that offers, issues
or renews a health insurance policy in this Commonwealth may not
unilaterally modify, amend or reinterpret any material term or
condition of a contract with a health care provider during the
term of the contract.
(b) Exception.--An insurer or administrator acting on behalf
of an insurer may implement changes to a contract with a health
care provider:
(1) after the mutual written agreement of the insurer or
administrator and the health care provider; or
(2) if the change is expressly required by Federal or
State law.
(c) Written notice.--A change not required by law may not
take effect until the renewal of the contract term and then only
if the insurer or administrator has provided written notice of
the proposed change at least 90 days before the renewal of the
contract.
(d) Penalties.--A violation of this chapter shall be deemed
a violation of the act of December 17, 1968 (P.L.1224, No.387),
known as the Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law,
and the act of July 22, 1974 (P.L.589, No.205), known as the
Unfair Insurance Practices Act. The department shall have the
authority to impose a civil penalty of up to $1,000 on an
insurer or administrator that violates this chapter and to
impose any other penalties authorized by law.
(e) Construction.--Nothing in this section shall be
construed to:
(1) Prohibit an insurer or administrator and a health
care provider from mutually agreeing to modify, amend or
