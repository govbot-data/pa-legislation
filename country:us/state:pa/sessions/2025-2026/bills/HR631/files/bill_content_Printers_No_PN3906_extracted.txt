andWHEREAS, To become licensed as a respiratory therapist in this Commonwealth, an individual must complete an approved respiratory care program, an examination from the National Board for Respiratory Care and, every two years, a minimum of 30 hours of continuing education for licensure renewal; andWHEREAS, Demand for respiratory therapists is increasing as the United States population continues to age; andWHEREAS, Further, estimates from the American Lung Association show that more than 35 million individuals in the United States live with a chronic lung disease and almost 44 1234567891011121314151617 million cases of some type of an acute respiratory disease are diagnosed every year; andWHEREAS, The United States Bureau of Labor Statistics estimates an employment growth rate of 9% for respiratory therapists from 2025 to 2035, which is considered much faster than average; andWHEREAS, Respiratory therapists care for patients of all ages, from premature infants requiring life-sustaining support to critically ill patients requiring advanced airway management and mechanical ventilation; andWHEREAS, Common conditions that a respiratory therapist may treat during their work include acute respiratory failure, asthma, bronchitis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and sleep apnea, among many other acute and chronic conditions; andWHEREAS, Respiratory therapists are integral members of the health care team, providing lifesaving interventions and expertise in emergency and critical care, neonatal and pediatric care, medical and surgical services, pulmonary diagnostics, sleep medicine, pulmonary rehabilitation and home care; andWHEREAS, Respiratory therapists use their expertise in airway management, mechanical ventilation, oxygen therapy, pulmonary function testing, medication delivery, rehabilitation and patient education to improve outcomes and quality of life; andWHEREAS, Respiratory therapists bring clinical expertise, compassion and calm during some of the most critical moments in a patient's life, when every breath matters; andWHEREAS, Respiratory therapists deserve recognition for their service and commitment to helping Pennsylvanians breathe and live better;
