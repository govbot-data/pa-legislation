§ 501. Construction.
This chapter shall be construed in pari materia with the act
of October 27, 1955 (P.L.744, No.222), known as the Pennsylvania
Human Relations Act, and shall not be interpreted to diminish a
right or remedy available under Federal or State law, including
20 U.S.C. Ch. 52 Subch. VIII (relating to equal access) and the
act of December 9, 2002 (P.L.1701, No.214), known as the
Religious Freedom Protection Act. Nothing in this chapter shall
be construed to restrict, limit or diminish any student or
school employee's right to religious or nonreligious expression
not addressed by this chapter.
§ 502. Definitions.
The following words and phrases when used in this chapter
shall have the meanings given to them in this section unless the
context clearly indicates otherwise:
"Academic study of religion." The objective, secular
instruction concerning religion as a subject of history,
literature, culture or social studies, presented without
endorsement or denigration and consistent with academic
standards, including comparative religion, history of religion
and the role of religion in literature, culture, art and music.
"Coerce." The exertion of direct or indirect pressure that
would cause a reasonable student, of similar age and in similar
circumstances, to participate in or refrain from religious
activity against conscience.

"Commission." The Pennsylvania Human Relations Commission.
"Consent." A voluntary, affirmative and informed agreement
that may be terminated at any time for any reason by a student
to participate in a religious activity, given freely without
coercion, pressure, inducement or fear of retaliation. The term
does not include an inference from silence, attendance at a
school event or prior participation or compliance with the
authority of a school official.
"Covered person." Any of the following individuals or
persons that allege a violation of this chapter:
(1) A student, parent or guardian acting on behalf of a
minor student.
(2) A school employee, including an individual who
contracts to perform a function for a school.
(3) A student organization registered with or seeking
recognition from a school.
"Devotional expression." Communication that is addressed to
a deity or that invites, leads or directs an audience to engage
in an act of worship, prayer or religious ritual.
"Graduation or promotion ceremony." A school event marking
completion of a grade level, program or educational milestone,
including a commencement exercise, promotion ceremony, award
ceremony and a ceremony that is substantially similar in purpose
or function.
"Instructional time." A portion of the school day when a
student is engaged in required curricular instruction under the
direct supervision of a school official teaching a curriculum.
The term does not include time before or after the official
school day, lunch, recess, a period between classes, study hall
or other noncurricular break.

§ 504. Prohibited acts.
(a) Acting in official capacity.--
(1) In determining whether a school official acted in an
official capacity, the totality of the objective
circumstances shall be considered, including:
(i) whether the school created, commissioned or
selected the specific communication of the school
official;
(ii) whether the school official was performing an
assigned instructional, supervisory or ceremonial role;
(iii) whether the school controlled or approved the
content of the communication;
(iv) whether the school official participated in an
activity in which students were a captive audience
subject to the school official's authority;
(v) whether the school official communicated to
students through an official school communication
channel; and
(vi) whether the school official was on personal or
noninstructional time.
(2) A school official does not act in an official
capacity under paragraph (1) if the school official makes a
genuinely personal expression, regardless of whether the
school official is present on school property or otherwise on
duty.

(b) Accommodation.--
(1) A school shall provide reasonable accommodation of a
student's religious practice upon request, unless:
(i) the school demonstrates that doing so would
materially disrupt operations or compromise safety; or
(ii) a request implicates a substantial burden on
religious exercise and the school can demonstrate that
denial is the least restrictive means of furthering a
compelling governmental interest, consistent with the act
of December 9, 2002 (P.L.1701, No.214), known as the
Religious Freedom Protection Act.
(2) An accommodation provided under this subsection,
including the assignment of space in a school facility, time
or routine supervision, does not by itself render the
accommodation a school-sponsored activity.
(3) A request under this subsection may be made by a
student or by a parent or guardian on behalf of a student
under 18 years of age.
(4) A school shall decide a request within a reasonable
time on events that are not imminent and on an expedited
basis where the request concerns an imminent event.
(5) If a school denies a request, the school shall state
the reasons for any denial in writing and include whether a
less restrictive alternative was considered.
(6) A school may not retaliate against a student, parent
or guardian for making a request under this subsection.

§ 506. Enforcement by commission.
(a) Violation.--A violation of this chapter constitutes an
unlawful discriminatory practice under the act of October 27,
1955 (P.L.744, No.222), known as the Pennsylvania Human
Relations Act.
(b) Administrative enforcement.--A complaint alleging a
violation of this chapter must be filed with, and shall be
adjudicated by, the commission in accordance with the
Pennsylvania Human Relations Act. The commission shall process a
complaint under this chapter on an expedited basis consistent
with regulation promulgated under section 509 (relating to
regulations) and may award the following relief:
(1) A cease-and-desist order.
(2) An affirmative compliance order.
(3) An order directing correction or expungement of
school records.
(4) An order restoring organizational recognition or
standing.

§ 510. Severability.
If any provision of this chapter or its application to any
person or circumstance is held invalid, the invalidity does not
affect other provisions or applications of this chapter which
can be given effect without the invalid provision or application
and to this end the provisions of this chapter are declared
severable.
Section 2. This act shall take effect as follows:
(1) The following shall take effect immediately:
The addition of 24 Pa.C.S. § 509.
This section.
(2) The remainder of this act shall take effect 90 days
after the publication of regulations under 24 Pa.C.S. § 509,
or in one year, whichever is earlier.
