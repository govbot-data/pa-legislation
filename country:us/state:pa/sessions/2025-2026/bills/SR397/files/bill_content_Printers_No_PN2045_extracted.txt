WARD AND HUTCHINSON, OCTOBER 15, 2026 REFERRED TO HEALTH AND HUMAN SERVICES, OCTOBER 15, 2026 A RESOLUTIONDirecting the Legislative Budget and Finance Committee to conduct a study on the financial impact of outdated hospital regulations on hospitals in this Commonwealth.WHEREAS, The Senate recognizes the paramount importance of hospital regulations to ensure that patients receive safe, high-quality care; andWHEREAS, Hospital licensure regulations have not been updated in nearly 40 years and do not reflect the current operating environment; andWHEREAS, Hospital policies, as written, create a greater need to use an exception or waiver process, lead to inconsistent interpretations by the Department of Health surveyors and make it difficult for facilities to plan for new technologies or strategies to ensure health care access; andWHEREAS, Misalignment with Federal regulations imposes undue administrative burden on hospitals in this Commonwealth with no positive impact on patient safety; andWHEREAS, Pennsylvania hospitals expend significant resources, 123456789101112131415161718 both financial and staff, to ensure compliance with outdated regulations when those resources could be redirected back towards patient care; andWHEREAS, The Legislative Budget and Finance Committee, with its proven expertise in conducting impartial research, convening impacted stakeholders, providing objective analyses and drafting actionable recommendations, is uniquely positioned to undertake such an examination; therefore be itRESOLVED, That the Senate direct the Legislative Budget and Finance Committee to conduct a study examining the financial impact of outdated hospital regulations on hospitals in this Commonwealth; and be it furtherRESOLVED, That the study include, but not be limited to:(1) an analysis of the number of Federal and State agencies currently overseeing or regulating hospital operations and the number and nature of the regulations hospitals are subject to under each;(2) an analysis of the number or percentage of State regulations that are duplicative, outdated or unnecessary in the current operating environment;(3) an evaluation of the volume of resources currently expended by hospitals to comply with regulations, including the number of full-time equivalents, salaries, operational costs and construction costs;(4) an evaluation of the current sub-regulatory guidance, policy statements or other guidance documents that provide the framework for compliance enforcement;(5) an evaluation of oversight structures that involve several State agencies or several divisions within one State agency;
