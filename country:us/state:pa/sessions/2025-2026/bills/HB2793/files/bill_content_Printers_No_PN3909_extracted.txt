Short title.This act shall be known and may be cited as the Skill Game Temporary Transition Act.Section 2. Findings and declarations.The General Assembly finds and declares as follows:(1) On June 15, 2026, the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania held in In re Three Pennsylvania Skill Amusement Devices, 358 A.3d 385 (Pa. 2026), that certain skill game devices are subject to 4 Pa.C.S. Pt. II (relating to gaming) and 18 Pa.C.S. § 5513 (relating to gambling devices, gambling, etc.).(2) The Supreme Court of Pennsylvania stayed its order for 120 days in recognition of the reasonable reliance on prior case law regarding the lawfulness of skill game devices 123456789101112131415161718 and the potential disruption to business owners and other good faith participants in the skill game industry.(3) The 120-day period established by the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania expires October 13, 2026.(4) The General Assembly is considering legislation regarding the future of skill games in this Commonwealth, including regulation, taxation, licensing, enforcement, consumer protections and permissible locations for skill game devices.(5) The issues under paragraph (4) involve significant policy considerations that require additional legislative deliberation and consultation with affected stakeholders.(6) An abrupt change in the legal status of skill game devices before the General Assembly completes its consideration of a permanent statutory framework may have significant consequences for small businesses, social and veterans organizations, volunteer fire companies, employees, distributors, manufacturers and other persons that participated in the skill game industry under the legal framework existing before June 15, 2026.(7) The purpose of this act is to establish a limited and temporary transition period during which the General Assembly may continue to consider a permanent statutory framework governing skill games.(8) This act is not intended to establish or prejudge a permanent tax rate, licensing structure, regulatory framework, revenue distribution formula, location restriction or other element of a permanent skill game program.Section 3. Definitions.The following words and phrases when used in this act shall 20260HB2793PN3909 - 2 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 have the meanings given to them in this section unless the context clearly indicates otherwise:"Covered person." A person that, before June 15, 2026, owned, possessed, operated, maintained, distributed, manufactured or supplied a covered skill game device, permitted the operation of a covered skill game device at a location under the person's control or otherwise participated in the operation of a covered skill game device."Covered skill game device." A skill slot machine or hybrid slot machine, as those terms are defined in 4 Pa.C.S. § 1103 (relating to definitions), that, before June 15, 2026, was possessed, operated or maintained at a location in this Commonwealth other than a licensed facility, as defined in 4 Pa.C.S. § 1103."Transition period." The period beginning on the later of October 14, 2026, or the effective date of this definition, and ending at the end of April 13, 2027.Section 4. Temporary authorization.(a) Authorization.--During the transition period, notwithstanding 4 Pa.C.S. Pt. II (relating to gaming) and 18 Pa.C.S. § 5513(a) (relating to gambling devices, gambling, etc.), a covered person may possess, operate, maintain or distribute a covered skill game device or permit the operation of a covered skill game device at a location under the covered person's control.(b) Crimes Code.--An activity authorized under subsection (a) shall not constitute an offense under 18 Pa.C.S. § 5513(a).(c) Gaming.--A covered person shall not be required to obtain a license, permit, certificate, registration, approval or other authorization under 4 Pa.C.S. Enforcement.(a) Limitation.--During the transition period, a law enforcement agency, Commonwealth agency or municipality may not take enforcement action against a covered person solely for engaging in an activity authorized under section 4(a).(b) Other violations.--Nothing in this act shall be construed to prohibit the enforcement of a Federal or State law regarding conduct independent of an activity authorized under section 4(a), including a Federal or State law regarding theft, fraud, money laundering, public safety, underage gambling or other criminal conduct.Section 6. Construction.(a) Other laws.--Except as otherwise provided in this act, nothing in this act shall supersede or modify a provision of Federal or State law governing a covered skill game device or an activity involving a covered skill game device.(b) Permanent framework.--Nothing in this act shall be construed to establish a permanent authorization, tax rate, licensing or registration system, regulatory framework, enforcement authority, revenue collection or distribution formula, consumer protection, responsible gaming requirement, location restriction, age restriction or other requirement governing skill game devices.Section 7.
