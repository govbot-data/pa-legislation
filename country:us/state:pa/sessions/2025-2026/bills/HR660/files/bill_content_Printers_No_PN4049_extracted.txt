andWHEREAS, Magisterial district judges preside over criminal preliminary arraignments and preliminary hearings, summary proceedings, landlord-tenant matters, civil claims within their jurisdiction, protection from abuse proceedings as authorized by law and other matters that routinely bring judges and court employees into direct contact with criminal defendants, victims, witnesses, litigants and members of the public; andWHEREAS, Unlike county courthouses, magisterial district courts are dispersed throughout communities and operate in a wide variety of county-owned and leased facilities, including 1234567891011121314151617181920 stand-alone offices, municipal buildings, commercial properties and other locations that were not necessarily designed for modern court security needs; andWHEREAS, Because security for magisterial district courts is implemented principally at the county and judicial district level, the type and level of security available at individual courts can vary substantially based on facility design, staffing, local policy and available financial resources; andWHEREAS, Security threats at magisterial district courts are not theoretical and serious incidents have demonstrated the risks faced by judges, court employees, law enforcement officers, litigants, witnesses and members of the public; andWHEREAS, On September 19, 2018, a gunman opened fire at a Fayette County magisterial district court, wounding four people before the gunman was shot and killed by police; andWHEREAS, On June 28, 2019, a defendant fled a holding area at a Delaware County magisterial district court and was killed while attempting to wrestle a firearm from an officer in the parking lot; andWHEREAS, On December 3, 2019, a defendant shot and seriously injured himself in the waiting room of a Dauphin County magisterial district court; andWHEREAS, On November 17, 2021, at Blair County Central Court in Altoona, a Blair County Prison inmate who was present for a preliminary hearing assaulted Corrections Officer Rhonda Russell, seized her service weapon and took her hostage; during the response, Officer Russell was fatally struck by a shot fired by a responding Altoona police officer at the armed inmate; andWHEREAS, In March 2023, a defendant being arraigned at a Lancaster County magisterial district court on charges that 20260HR0660PN4049 - 2 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 included terroristic threats and criminal mischief doused himself with a flammable liquid, set himself on fire and ran toward law enforcement officers inside the court; andWHEREAS, Following the 2018 Fayette County shooting, Fayette County implemented full-time security screening at its magisterial district courts and began maintaining a log of weapons detected by court security personnel; andWHEREAS, Fayette County records show that court security personnel detected 795 weapons in 2019, 1,092 weapons in 2020, 943 weapons in 2021, 1,543 weapons in 2022, 1,393 weapons in 2023, 1,188 weapons in 2024 and 1,063 weapons in 2025, for a total of 8,017 weapons detected over the seven-year period from 2019 through 2025; andWHEREAS, The Court Administrator of Pennsylvania convened a Magisterial District Court Security Task Group in 2019 to review the security posture of magisterial district courts and recommend measures to improve the safety of the public, court staff and judges; andWHEREAS, In August 2020, an executive summary of the task group's work identified recommendations, including an armed court security officer during regular office hours, trained weapons screening, expanded use of advanced communication technology, stronger facility and security guidelines, consideration of minimum office security standards, stronger local court security oversight and emergency planning and annual security training; andWHEREAS, The 2020 review also recognized that meaningful implementation would require cooperation among the General Assembly, Governor, Judiciary and counties and encouraged identification of recurring funding for court security personnel and screening equipment; andWHEREAS, More than six years have elapsed since that Statewide review, during which time security incidents have continued, security technology and best practices have evolved, facility needs have changed and counties have had differing capacities to implement recommended improvements;
