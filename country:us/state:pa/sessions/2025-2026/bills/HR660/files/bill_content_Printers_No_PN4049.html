A RESOLUTION
Directing the Joint State Government Commission to conduct a
comprehensive study of security at magisterial district
courts in this Commonwealth, including an update of prior
findings and recommendations, an assessment of current
security conditions, practices, incidents and recommendations
for minimum security standards and implementation.
WHEREAS, Magisterial district courts are an integral part of
the Unified Judicial System of the Commonwealth and serve as the
first point of contact with Pennsylvania's judicial system for
many residents; and
WHEREAS, Magisterial district judges preside over criminal
preliminary arraignments and preliminary hearings, summary
proceedings, landlord-tenant matters, civil claims within their
jurisdiction, protection from abuse proceedings as authorized by
law and other matters that routinely bring judges and court
employees into direct contact with criminal defendants, victims,
witnesses, litigants and members of the public; and
WHEREAS, Unlike county courthouses, magisterial district
courts are dispersed throughout communities and operate in a
wide variety of county-owned and leased facilities, including

WHEREAS, Because security for magisterial district courts is
implemented principally at the county and judicial district
level, the type and level of security available at individual
courts can vary substantially based on facility design,
staffing, local policy and available financial resources; and
WHEREAS, Security threats at magisterial district courts are
not theoretical and serious incidents have demonstrated the
risks faced by judges, court employees, law enforcement
officers, litigants, witnesses and members of the public; and
WHEREAS, On September 19, 2018, a gunman opened fire at a
Fayette County magisterial district court, wounding four people
before the gunman was shot and killed by police; and
WHEREAS, On June 28, 2019, a defendant fled a holding area at
a Delaware County magisterial district court and was killed
while attempting to wrestle a firearm from an officer in the
parking lot; and
WHEREAS, On December 3, 2019, a defendant shot and seriously
injured himself in the waiting room of a Dauphin County
magisterial district court; and
WHEREAS, On November 17, 2021, at Blair County Central Court
in Altoona, a Blair County Prison inmate who was present for a
preliminary hearing assaulted Corrections Officer Rhonda
Russell, seized her service weapon and took her hostage; during
the response, Officer Russell was fatally struck by a shot fired
by a responding Altoona police officer at the armed inmate; and
WHEREAS, In March 2023, a defendant being arraigned at a

WHEREAS, Data from the Pennsylvania Judicial Incident
Reporting System has documented a persistent volume of security
incidents at magisterial district courts, including 109
incidents in the first quarter of 2026; and
WHEREAS, Following the 2018 Fayette County shooting, Fayette
County implemented full-time security screening at its
magisterial district courts and began maintaining a log of
weapons detected by court security personnel; and
WHEREAS, Fayette County records show that court security
personnel detected 795 weapons in 2019, 1,092 weapons in 2020,
943 weapons in 2021, 1,543 weapons in 2022, 1,393 weapons in
2023, 1,188 weapons in 2024 and 1,063 weapons in 2025, for a
total of 8,017 weapons detected over the seven-year period from
2019 through 2025; and
WHEREAS, The volume of weapons detected in Fayette County
after implementation of routine screening demonstrates both the
continuing frequency with which weapons and other dangerous
items are brought to magisterial district court facilities and
the value of consistent entrance screening in identifying those
items before they enter court areas; and
WHEREAS, A Statewide magisterial district court security
survey conducted in 2019 found that 93% of responding courts did
not screen the general public at the public entrance on a daily
basis; and
WHEREAS, The same Statewide review identified disparities in
security hardware, access controls, duress alarms, closed-
circuit television, ballistic protection, emergency planning,

WHEREAS, The Court Administrator of Pennsylvania convened a
Magisterial District Court Security Task Group in 2019 to review
the security posture of magisterial district courts and
recommend measures to improve the safety of the public, court
staff and judges; and
WHEREAS, In August 2020, an executive summary of the task
group's work identified recommendations, including an armed
court security officer during regular office hours, trained
weapons screening, expanded use of advanced communication
technology, stronger facility and security guidelines,
consideration of minimum office security standards, stronger
local court security oversight and emergency planning and annual
security training; and
WHEREAS, The 2020 review also recognized that meaningful
implementation would require cooperation among the General
Assembly, Governor, Judiciary and counties and encouraged
identification of recurring funding for court security personnel
and screening equipment; and
WHEREAS, More than six years have elapsed since that
Statewide review, during which time security incidents have
continued, security technology and best practices have evolved,
facility needs have changed and counties have had differing
capacities to implement recommended improvements; and
WHEREAS, A current, independent and comprehensive Statewide
assessment is therefore necessary to determine the extent to
which prior recommendations have been implemented, identify
remaining security gaps, establish current costs and needs and

RESOLVED, That the House of Representatives direct the Joint
State Government Commission to conduct a comprehensive study of
security at magisterial district courts in this Commonwealth,
including an update of prior findings and recommendations, an
assessment of current security conditions, practices, incidents
and recommendations for minimum security standards and
implementation; and be it further
RESOLVED, That, in conducting the study, the Joint State
Government Commission shall, at a minimum:
(1) compile a current county-by-county inventory of
security at magisterial district courts, identifying facility
type and ownership or lease status, front door security,
whether security personnel are armed, the provider and hours
of security, weapons screening practices and equipment and
other security-related policies and procedures;
(2) obtain and analyze available Pennsylvania Judicial
Incident Reporting System data and other incident data for
magisterial district courts from 2020 through the most recent
available date, including incidents involving threats,
assaults, weapons, contraband, disorderly conduct,
harassment, escape or attempted escape, property damage and
other conduct affecting court security;
(3) identify significant security incidents at
magisterial district courts and evaluate whether those
incidents reveal recurring vulnerabilities in screening,
facility design, prisoner control, staffing, emergency
response or other security practices;
